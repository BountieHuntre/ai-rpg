# AI RPG

3D pixel art action RPG built with Godot 4.6 (Forward+, Jolt physics).

## Running
- Editor/game: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64.exe`
- Console build, for logs and scripted checks: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64_console.exe`
- Run the game: `<console exe> --path C:/ai-rpg`
- Re-import after adding files: `<console exe> --headless --path C:/ai-rpg --import`

## Layout
- `scenes/<area>/`: scenes with their scripts beside them (`main`, `player`, `enemies`, `levels`, `camera`)
- `scripts/combat/`: shared combat logic (`Combatant`, `AbilityCaster`, `Targeting`, `Ability`)
- `data/abilities/`: one `Ability` resource per ability
- `scripts/stats/`, `scripts/items/`, `data/classic/`: classic WoW 1.12 stats, combat table, XP, creatures, items and loot, with exact tables and formulas from the CMaNGOS and vmangos server code (see `data/classic/SOURCE.md`). Keep new rules faithful to classic and note the source. `tools/classic_db/extract.py` regenerates the JSON from the CMaNGOS classic database.
- Spells: `SpellDB` reads `data/classic/spells.json`; `SpellCaster` (on the Player) casts with classic checks, GCD, cooldowns and cast times, and holds each spell's effect code following CMaNGOS. Buffs are `Combatant.auras`. `GameState.known_spells` and `action_bar` are saved. UI: `ui/spell_ui.gd` (action bar 1-0,-,=; spellbook P, laid out as classic's: pages of twelve spells in two columns, with tabs down its right edge for General and each talent tree, a spell's tab coming from its skill line (client SkillLineAbility, `skill_lines.json` from `fetch_client_tables.py`, exported as `book_tabs`, `SpellDB.book_tab`), and a "Hide old ranks" box showing only each spell's highest known rank, saved as the `spellbook_hide_old_ranks` option); buff bar). The target frame shows the target's buffs and debuffs underneath (debuffs are what the other side put on it; the player's own framed gold; hover for what they do), with its cast bar below them. Tooltips never catch the mouse and sit left of the cursor where there's no room to the right. Four extra action bars can be turned on under Interface (`bar_bottom_left`, `bar_bottom_right`, `bar_right`, `bar_right_2`; `GameState.EXTRA_BARS`): the bottom two stack above the XP bar (the stance and cast bars move up), the right two are columns under the minimap (slots shrink to fit, and the quest tracker and bags move left). Their slots follow the main bar's in `GameState.action_bar` (60 in all, older saves padded), and their buttons are key binding actions `bottom_left_1`... (unbound by default, added to the InputMap by `Settings`); `GameState.slot_action`/`shown_slots` map slots to actions and to the bars shown. New spells go in the first empty slot of a shown bar. Spells are learned from an `Npc` with a `trainer` via `ui/trainer_window.gd` (Train, or Train All for everything available while the money lasts): Sir Aldric Fenn trains Templars ("paladin"), Sister Maud Ellery Clerics ("priest"), Sergeant Bram Holloway (by the smithy) Men-at-Arms ("warrior"); a trainer only teaches its own class (`SpellDB.TRAINER_CLASSES`, which also says which classes have spells). Godric Vane (the Weapon Master, by the smithy) teaches every class the weapon skills it may learn, 10 silver each (Polearms 1 gold from level 20): classic's weapon masters' lists joined (the `weapons` trainer, `WEAPON_MASTERS` in `extract.py`; each entry's `classes` mask from SkillRaceClassInfo, `weapon_skills.json`, which `SpellDB.trainer_list` filters by). Learning any proficiency spell grants its item subclasses (`SpellDB.proficiencies_of`), skills you started with show as known, and weapon skills stay out of the spellbook (`SpellDB.is_weapon_skill`). Bots know every weapon skill their class may learn at their level (`Bot.learn_spells`), so a Blademastery build's BiS is a two-hander. The Cleric list merges classic's five racial priest trainers (`CLASS_TRAINERS` in `extract.py`); racial spells carry `races` and `SpellDB.trainer_list(trainer, race)` leaves out other races'. Every Man-at-Arms, Templar and Cleric spell works except those needing another player (`NEEDS_PARTY`) or a missing system (`NOT_YET`: Dominate Will, Borrowed Eyes, Font of Light); racial first ranks and class-quest spells (Bastion and Fury Stance, Goad, Revival's first rank) are sold free (`RACIAL_FIRST_RANKS`, `QUEST_SPELLS` in `extract.py`); abilities that are talents come from the talent (the trainer sells their later ranks). The Man-at-Arms (classic Warrior) is in `scripts/spells/warrior_abilities.gd`, a child of the SpellCaster: rage (`Combatant.power_type`; rage spells' costs are tenths; earned from white hits dealt and damage taken by CMaNGOS's RewardRage formula, fading 3 a tick out of combat, never refilled like mana), stances (shapeshift auras on the stance bar, each with its passive via `SPELL_LINKS`; changing stance loses rage; new Men-at-Arms start in Vanguard Stance; abilities need the stances in their `Stances` mask), next-swing attacks (`Combatant.swing_replacer`), Rush and Cut Off, reactive strikes (Riposte Strike after a dodge, Retort after you avoid), shields, area attacks, shouts and taunts (`Enemy.taunt`, `AURA_MOD_TAUNT`). Two-Weapon Fighting lets one-handers go in the off hand, which swings on its own timer for half damage with classic's dual-wield miss. Spells another spell casts that the data doesn't link (Blessed Burst's heal, Withering Touch's and Umbral Guard's strikes) are `SPELL_LINKS` there (`SpellDB.linked_spell`). `SpellCaster` handles effects by kind (heals, damage of any school, buffs, debuffs with a hit roll, channels that moving or another spell ends, absorbs, fear that makes creatures flee, dispels) with the odd ones by name; the matching aura types (stats, dodge, speed, threat, aggro range, damage and healing percentages, heal/leech/trigger ticks) are read in `Combatant`, `CharacterStats` and `Enemy`. Combat-driven effects (Oath procs at classic procs-per-minute, Verdict debuffs that heal or restore, damage shields, hallowed ground, Umbral Guard and Withering Touch striking back, Inner Flame charges, Leeching Embrace) are in `scripts/spells/spell_procs.gd`; periodic aura ticks in `Combatant`. New ranks replace older ranks on the action bar (unlike classic). Class buffs lasting 5 minutes or more in classic (Boons and Greater Boons, Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit, Inner Flame, Shadow Warding, Zealous Fervor...) last an hour (`SpellDB._lengthen_buffs`, unlike classic; short ones such as Boon of Shelter and Rallying Cry keep theirs). "Boons" and "Greater Boons" are flyouts (`SpellDB.FLYOUTS`, ids no classic spell uses): spellbook entries that lead their tab and go on the action bar like spells, and clicking one (or its key, `Player.flyout_pressed`) opens a row of the boons you know, highest ranks, to cast one (`SpellUI._toggle_flyout`; upward from bottom bars, leftward from right ones, right-click in the spellbook). Templar Presences (classic paladin auras) get their own stance bar above the action bar, filled automatically in the order first learned. Stances, forms and Presences also show in the buff bar like any buff; never hide them there (only passive spells' auras, such as a stance's "Stance Passive" part, are left out). The stance bar frames the active one in gold. Tooltip descriptions for spells (and item Use:/Equip: lines) come from `scripts/spells/spell_text.gd`, written in the game's own words with numbers from the spell data; give a new spell a line there.
- Talents: classic's trees for the classes with spells, from the client's Talent and TalentTab tables (`fetch_client_tables.py` writes `talents.json`; `extract.py` exports every rank's spell and renames the trees: Man-at-Arms Blademastery, Ferocity, Bulwark; Templar Grace, Aegis, Zeal; Cleric Devotion, Mercy, Umbra). `scripts/spells/talents.gd` has the rules: a point a level from 10, 5 points in a tree per tier, prerequisites; ranks are `CharacterProfile.talents` (saved; `Talents.validate` on load), unlearning at the class trainer costs 1g, 5g, 10g, then 5g more each time to 50g (`talent_reset_cost`, saved), and the `resettalents` command is free. Talent abilities join the known spells; passive talents become permanent auras (`Combatant.add_passive_aura`, `aura.talent`), their highest rank only, while their weapon, shield or stance requirement holds (`Talents.sync_passives`, run by `CharacterStats.refresh`). Talents that change other spells are spell modifiers (`scripts/spells/spell_mods.gd`, CMaNGOS `ApplySpellMod`: `SpellDB.effect_value`/`cooldown`/`duration`/`cast_time`/`spell_range`/`effect_radius`/`mana_cost` take an optional caster; crit, damage, pushback, resist, crit bonus and charges are applied where those happen); a modifier with no mask of its own takes `spell_affect`'s, and later ranks take the first rank's. Combat events are `Combatant.proc_event` (CMaNGOS ProcFlags and procEx, with the spell); `scripts/spells/talent_procs.gd` fires talents' triggered spells from them (the first rank's `spell_proc_event` row covers every rank), with Lingering Wounds, Inner Light, Hallowed Recovery, Tooth for a Tooth and Gloomweave written out, and uses up talent buffs' charges. Stat talents are read in `CharacterStats`, `Combatant` and `CombatRules`. Descriptions are worked out in `scripts/spells/talent_text.gd`. The window is `ui/talent_window.gd` (N, the `talents` action). Bots spend their points down a main tree (`Talents.bot_trees`, `spend_for_bot`), skipping `BOT_SKIPS` (Lingering Spirit and Font of Light aren't in the game yet).
- NPCs: `scenes/npcs/npc.tscn` with `trainer` and/or `vendor` (a creature entry in `data/classic/vendors.json`). Vendors buy, sell (right-click bag items) and buy back, following CMaNGOS. Food and drink are item "Use:" spells handled by `SpellCaster.use_item`; X sits.
- Names are the game's own, not classic's: `tools/classic_db/renames.py` maps every classic item, creature, NPC and spell name to an original one when the data is exported (plain words like Wolf and Human stay). Class names are the game's own too (`ClassicData.CLASS_NAMES`: Man-at-Arms, Templar, Ranger, Cutpurse, Cleric, Seer, Magus, Hexer, Wildwarden); code and data keep classic's names for the enum and the "paladin" trainer. Code that spots spell kinds by name uses the new patterns: `Oath of` seals, `Verdict` judgements, `Boon of` blessings, `... Presence` auras.
- World: `scenes/levels/world.tscn` (`World`, with the sky and sun) holds the zones side by side in one world, in world coordinates, so bots walk between them; `World.zone_at(point)` says which zone a point is in (the one whose square holds it), and `height_at`, `places()`, `spawns()` and `enclosures()` answer for all of them. Each zone is a `Zone` (`scenes/levels/zone.gd`: its name, center, size, graveyard, hub, places, spawns and enclosures, plus shared ground, water, edge walls, clutter, torch, plank and creature-spawning helpers), built in code from the layout constants in its script, its NPCs in its `.tscn`. Zones have mountains along their edges from the same noise (`Zone.base_height`, `mountain_height`), so where two meet the ground joins exactly; `Zone.PASSES` are lines cut through the mountains on both sides (`carve_passes`) where the edge walls open. `scenes/levels/ashford_vale/` is the starting area (levels 1-6, centered on the origin): terrain from a height function (`scenes/levels/terrain.gd`), stream, roads, the priory, mine, brigand camp, bridge, seeded trees and rocks (`scenes/levels/props.gd`), and creature spawn groups. `scenes/levels/harrowfield/` (levels 6-12, centered 400 yards south) is farmland over the pass south of the vale's bridge: the village of Wexford (inn, chapel and churchyard, Marshal Edric Thorne), the Coombe farm and its pumpkin patch, the Wainwright cottage, the Old Delve mine, the Mirrow Mere, a brigand farmstead, the south watch and bridge, and the gnoll hills with Grunmaw's camp; its creatures and quests are classic Elwynn Forest's. Crossing into a zone shows its name (`main.gd` `_check_zone`). Saves store the zone; the dead come back at the graveyard of the zone they died in (`Zone.graveyard`: the priory, Wexford's churchyard), and anything else starts at `AshfordVale.SPAWN`. Enemy node names come from each zone's spawn layout (with a prefix per zone), so changing `SPAWNS` orphans saved corpses (harmless). `tele <zone>` goes to a zone's graveyard.
- Quests: `QuestDB` joins each quest's classic rules (`data/classic/quests.json`, exported from quest_template for the ids in `extract.py`'s `QUESTS`) with the game's own title, text, giver and ender (`data/quests.json`; givers are NPC node names). `QuestLog` has the rules (taking, kill credit, quest-only drops, turning in, classic quest XP); progress is `GameState.quests` and `quests_done`, saved. UI: `ui/quest_window.gd` (talking to an NPC with quests), `ui/quest_log_ui.gd` (L), the tracker and the "!"/"?" markers in `CombatHud`. To add a quest, add its classic entry to `QUESTS`, re-export, and write its text in `data/quests.json`.
- Enemies are set by `creature_entry` (a classic creature_template id); their stats, loot, money, respawn time and wander distance come from the data. Their placeholder looks are in `Enemy.LOOKS`. Whether they attack on sight comes from their classic faction (`data/classic/factions.json`, rules in `ClassicData`): neutral ones and hostile ones flagged no-aggro only fight back, and all passive creatures show yellow. A zone can make creatures passive regardless (`Enemy.passive`; Ashford Vale's `PASSIVE_CREATURES` covers every burrowling). Creatures cast their classic spells from their EventAI script (`creature_ai_scripts`, exported as each creature's `Events`; only cast and ranged-mode actions): `scenes/enemies/creature_ai.gd` (`Enemy.ai`, made for creatures with events) runs the timed, aggro, spawn, range, missing-buff and behind-the-target events, casts with cast times, mana (or a refilling 100 energy), cooldowns, pushback and missile travel, scales damage and mana cost to the creature's level (`creature_class_damage.json`, from `creature_template_classlevelstats`), and is interrupted by stuns, fear, silence (`PreventionType`) and interrupt effects (`Combatant.interrupt_cast`, which locks the school; Hilt Strike and Shield Smash call it). Casters in ranged mode stand off and cast their main spell, closing to melee when out of mana, silenced or locked out. The target frame shows the target's cast (`CombatHud.unit_cast`). Ashford Vale's casters: Burrowling Firecaller (Flame Bolt, Rime Shroud), Pickman (Prying Strike), Brigand Footpad (Back Thrust from behind), Ridgetusk Boar (Headlong Charge); Harrowfield adds the Brigand Hedge-Mage (Rime Bolt, Rime Shroud), Brigand Cutthroat (Snap Kick), Mirefin Lurker (Back Thrust), Mirefin Forager (a potion at low health), the spiders' poison, the Ditchjaw Outrider's Thrash and Grunmaw (Prying Strike, Head Butt), and gnolls and Brasstooth flee at low health. Creature auras that fire on their own melee hits (ProcFlags done melee: Poison Proc, Thrash) are `CreatureAI._on_proc_event`; ones that strike back at attackers are `_on_melee_hit_taken`. To add one, put its creature_template id in `extract.py`'s `CREATURES` and name it and its spells in `renames.py`. Pulling a creature calls for help (CMaNGOS `MarkCallAssistanceOnPull`): creatures of its faction within its `CallForHelp` radius (5 yards by default) that see it, aren't fighting and attack on sight join 2 s later, and their pulls can bring more (`Enemy._mark_assistance`); passive and neutral creatures never come. In Ashford Vale only the brigands do. EventAI can also make a creature flee in a panic for 10 s at low health (`Enemy.flee`, "... tries to run away in fear!") or call for help in a radius (`Enemy.call_for_help`); no vale creature has those in the data. Creatures the data has speak (on aggro, mostly) say lines of the game's own (`CreatureAI.SAYINGS`), in the chat as creature speech (`Chat.monster`, the "Creature Speech" filter).
- Playerbots: computer-run player characters (`scenes/bots/bot.gd`, made by `BotManager` from the Playerbots options). The player and bots are both `PlayerUnit`s, and each has a `CharacterProfile` (name, race, class, level, XP, gear, known spells; `GameState` extends it for the player), which `CharacterStats`, `Progression` and `SpellCaster` read, so bots follow the same rules and cast the same spells (`PlayerUnit.enemy_target()`/`friendly_target()` pick the targets). On their own, bots do quests (the "Random Bots Do Quests" option): `BotQuests` (`scenes/bots/bot_quests.gd`) is a bot's own quest log with the player's classic rules, up to three at a time, saved in its roster entry; bots walk to givers to take and hand in quests (quest XP for their level), hunt the creatures their quests need first, count their own kills, and roll each creature's classic drop chance for quest items (`LootTable.drop_chance`), since they don't loot. Otherwise they hunt creatures near their level, eat and drink when low, and get up 30 s after dying (at their zone's graveyard when on their own). They go in and out of walled places by the gate (the zones' enclosures) and between zones by the pass, joining it where it's nearest (`BotManager.route`); quests, quest givers and hunting grounds in either zone draw them across, so bots past the vale's levels head for Harrowfield. Far bots' long steps stop on each waypoint rather than overshooting it. Delivery quests' items (a letter, a pie) count as had once taken. Bots interrupt casting creatures (Hilt Strike, Shield Smash, Hush). Templar bots heal, keep a Presence, an Oath and Boons up, and use Revival. Man-at-Arms bots take a stance (Bastion with a shield in a party, else Vanguard), Rush in, shout Rallying Cry in fights, and use finishers, reactive strikes, Gash, area attacks when crowded and Mighty Blow with spare rage. Cleric bots heal (a Ward first when someone is in trouble, Communal Mending for three hurt), cure diseases, raise the fallen, keep Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit and their own buffs up, and fight from 25 yards with Gnawing Shadow, Mind Lash and Chastise while they have mana. The "Classes With Spells" bot class option makes only Men-at-Arms, Templars and Clerics. The roster is `playerbots.json` in the profile folder, shared by all characters: name, race, class, level, XP, its equipment (put back over its starting gear on spawn, `BotManager._restore_equipment`), and each bot's state (`Bot.saved_state()`: zone, position, facing, health, mana, death, a Stay order, its own buffs), saved with the game, so bots carry on where they were. Creatures keep a classic threat table (`Enemy.threat`, heals draw half their amount) and the first player unit to hit one taps it (`tapped_by`); only the player's group gets XP, quest credit and loot from its taps.
- Bot performance: bots more than 45 yards from the player (`Bot.FAR`, not in the party or a trade) are ticked by the `BotManager` in six slices (about 10 times a second each, `Bot.far_tick`) with their nodes' own per-frame work switched off (`Bot.set_far`), and walk the ground's height without collision; they think less often, and re-check quest needs and quest givers every 2 s. Creatures look for players to attack 5 times a second. The Random Bots option goes up to 250: measured on the dev machine, 200 bots hold 65-70 fps, 250 about 55-60, 300 about 40, and from 400 the physics falls behind and the game drops under 10 fps.
- Party: the `Party` autoload holds up to four bots with the player (`GameState.party` saves their names). Clicking a bot in the world only targets it; right-click the target frame (or a party frame, or its name in chat) for its menu: whisper, invite, trade, or tell a member to follow or stay. Bots keep their own level in the party. With "Teleport to Me When Left Behind" on, a following bot teleports beside you when it's over 40 yards away (even mid-fight) or has gained no ground for 4 s while over 12 yards back; bots told to Stay don't. Quest XP the player earns goes to every party bot too, the same amount (`Party.share_quest_xp`, unlike classic). Kill XP is shared by `Group::RewardGroupAtKill` rules (members alive within 74 yards, by level, with the group bonus), and looted money is split. Party frames are under the player frame, with each member's health, mana and XP; members show on the minimap. The target frame shows a mana bar for targets with mana (creatures have their classic mana).
- Chat: the `Chat` autoload holds the log (kept across the reload after death, not saved) and classic's colors and channels (1. General, 2. Trade, 3. LocalDefense, 4. LookingForGroup; General and LocalDefense are per zone, though the tag doesn't name it); `Chat.say` only reaches the player within say (25 yd) or yell (300 yd) range. `ui/chat_frame.gd` is the box in the bottom-left, with tabs: General (every channel, can't be closed) plus any the player adds with "+"; right-click a tab for New Tab, Edit Tab (name and which kinds of line it shows, `ChatFrame.CATEGORIES`), Close Tab and Lock/Unlock Chat; unlocked, drag a tab to move it and the corner grip to resize it. Tabs, place, size and lock are saved as `chatframe_tabs`, `chatframe_rect` and `chatframe_locked` in the options. Enter to type, Esc to cancel, Up/Down for history, sticky channels, "/p " and the like switch the channel as you type, click a name to whisper, right-click it for the bot's menu (as on its frame). Links: shift-click an item in the bags or character window, a quest in the quest log, or a link in the chat to put "[Name]" in the line; any "[Name]" naming a real item or a quest's title is linked (`Chat.link_text`), items in their quality color with their tooltip on hover, quests in their difficulty color with a tooltip of level, objective, quest giver and your progress (`ChatFrame.quest_tooltip`). A line's links travel as its `links` ({ name: "item:id" or "quest:id" }); bots write items and quests in brackets, so theirs link too. Slash commands: /s /y /p /1-/4 /w /r /e, emotes (/wave...), /who, /invite, /leave, /help; any other slash runs a developer console command (`DevConsole.COMMANDS`) with its result in the chat. Loot, party and system messages go to the chat too. Bots talk through `scenes/bots/bot_chatter.gd` (the "Bot Chat" option). Every line comes from what the bot is doing (`_things_to_say`): its quest and where that takes it, the named place it's in (the zone's `PLACES`, via `BotManager.place_at`) and the creatures there, what last killed it and where (`Bot.last_death`), the quest it just handed in, items its last kill drops; answers to questions about places, creatures, quests and trainers come from the zone's layout. Also "ding" on levelling, greetings when joining, answers to the player's say, party chat and whispers, waving back, and "follow"/"stay" orders in party chat.
- Trading: `ui/trade_window.gd`, opened from a bot's menu (Trade), within 11.11 yards. Classic rules: six slots and money a side, both accept, any change takes your acceptance back, soulbound items can't go, and it ends if either side is attacked or walks off. Right-click a bag item (or drop the held one on a slot) to offer it, right-click a slot to take it back, type money as "1s 50c". Bots carry a `BotBag` (`scenes/bots/bot_bag.gd`, saved in the roster): tradeable loot and money from their own kills, 12 stacks, trash and then the cheapest vendored when full. Grey (poor quality) items, equipment included, are trash (`BotBag.is_trash`): bots never offer them, pay only the vendor price for them, and sell them when they pass within 15 yards of a vendor. A trade starts with the item last talked about in chat (`BotChatter.trade_topic`: its ad, a quote, an agreed price) already in: on the bot's side, as many as were talked about, when it's selling, and from your bags when it's buying (`TradeWindow._put_in_topic`). A panel beside the window lists what it sells at its own markup (2-4x vendor price), and clicking one puts it in (clicking it in the trade puts it back). It buys yours at its own rate (1.1-1.4x vendor price), puts in the difference when it owes you, and accepts once your money covers what it wants. Its Trade chat sells what's really in its bag. Trade talk (`BotChatter` "Trade talk"): "WTS [Item]" in a channel or out loud brings whispered offers from a couple of bots, "WTB [Item]" a price from a bot that has it; whispering a bot "how much would you pay for [Item]?", "what do you have?" or a price ("3s?") gets a quote, a list, or an accept/counter (it pays up to 1.6x vendor, sells down to 1.5x). Whispering about its ad without a link ("i'll buy it", "how much?", "still have it?") gets its price for that item, and a linked item with "buy", "want" or "sell me" is the player buying (only "pay", "give", "offer", "worth" or "sell you" make it a sale to the bot; `BotChatter._player_buying`/`_player_selling`). A bare price is about the bot's trade topic (`_topic`: the item last linked to it or in its own last ad, kept 5 minutes; "buy"/"sell" in the line say which way), and is for the lot unless it says "each" or only makes sense per item; with no topic the bot asks which item. Quoted and agreed prices are kept in `BotBag.quotes` and used by the trade window, which updates if a trade with that bot is open. A bot further away than trade range asks "want me to come to you?" with its offer (buying or selling); a yes, or agreeing a price, sends it walking over (`Bot.come_to_player`), and on arrival it opens the trade.
- Social window: `ui/social_window.gd` (O, the `social` action). Friends tab (`GameState.friends`, saved per character; name, level, class, zone or Offline; add by name, remove, invite) and Who tab (every playerbot in the player's zone with level, race and class, a search box, add friend, invite).
- Minimap: `ui/minimap.gd`, top right, north up. It renders the zone the player is in once from above (units hidden) into a texture cached per zone, swapping to the next zone's map and name on crossing into it; quest givers' markers and the spawn areas of creatures that active quests still need, in any zone (on the rim when off the map), are drawn over it, and creatures made by `spawn` as red dots (bigger for elites and bosses, grey when dead, on the rim when off the map; hover for name and level). Buffs sit to its left and the quest tracker below it (`Minimap.corner`).
- Options: the `Settings` autoload holds the Video, Sound, Interface, Chat (text size, background, outline; `ChatFrame` reads them and the HUD rebuilds on a `chat_` change; its size is dragged) and Playerbots options and the key bindings (`Settings.ACTIONS`, saved in the `[keys]` section, applied to the InputMap; new rebindable actions go in that list) (`options.cfg` in the profile folder, or beside the `--save=` file), applies display, window size, monitor, frame caps (Background Frame Rate when unfocused) and sound itself, and emits `changed(key)` for the rest. The world's video options (`Settings.WORLD_OPTIONS`: shadow quality, view distance with its fog, field of view, Environment Detail, ambient occlusion, bloom, brightness, gamma as a color-correction curve, contrast, saturation) are applied by `main.gd`'s `_apply_setting`; Environment Detail sets how much of the zones' ground clutter (grass and flowers, no collision; `World.set_clutter`) is drawn. Show Frame Rate is a counter by the minimap. The Options button (Esc menu, login and character select) opens `ui/options_window.gd`, one window with a tab per category; changes (key bindings included) wait in the window until Apply (applies and saves) or Okay (the same, and closes), and Cancel or Esc drops them; add an option to `Settings.DEFAULTS` and a row to `OptionsWindow.CATEGORIES`, then read it where it matters.
- Version: the game's version is `config/version` in `project.godot` (`[application]`), as x.y.z, each part any number of digits (0.1.0, 0.12.105). The login screen shows it and Windows exports take it (padded to x.y.z.0; Windows caps each part at 65535), so it is set nowhere else. It changes only when a release is made (a build for players, when the user asks for one), not on every push: the release commit bumps it, x for major updates (a new zone, a big new system), y for moderate ones (a new class, a feature like creature spellcasting), z for small ones (fixes, tweaks, options, dev tools), judged by the largest change since the last release. Before bumping it, propose the new number (and why: the largest change) and wait for the user to confirm it or pick another. Bumping a part resets the parts after it to 0 (0.4.7 to 0.5.0). The release also adds an entry for its new version at the top of `data/patch_notes.json` (`version`, `date` as MM-DD-YYYY, `notes`: short lines for players, in the game's own names) covering everything since the last release (`git log` since the commit that last changed `config/version`); the login screen shows only that newest entry. Ordinary pushes leave both alone.
- Release builds: `export_presets.cfg` has a Windows preset (it needs Godot 4.6's export templates installed). It includes `*.json` and `default_options.cfg`, which exports leave out otherwise; the game reads its data from those. `default_options.cfg` (res://) holds the shipped defaults, read over `Settings.DEFAULTS` in every build (players' own `options.cfg` still wins, and Defaults buttons go back to it). `shipdefaults [keys | clear]` in the dev console or chat writes the current options into it (editor runs only; `monitor` and the chat's placement aren't shipped). `Settings.developer_tools()` is false in release exports: no console, Developer options tab, developer chat commands or Shift+F12, and the `--save=`/`--profile=`/`--resume=` arguments are ignored. `-- --pretend-release` tries this from the editor (test arguments still work).

## Conventions
- Typed GDScript, tabs, tunable values as `@export`.
- The 3D game renders in `GameView/GameViewport` at a whole-number pixel scale, at least `min_game_height` rows tall (set on the `Main` node). The look depends on this low resolution.
- The HUD draws at full window resolution, outside the game view. Its layout is in units of 1/270 of the window height, times the UI Scale option (`Settings.hud_scale`).
- Outlines come from `shaders/pixelart_stylizer.gdshader` (MIT, by Leo Peltola) on a plane attached to the game camera. It needs Forward+. Tune them in `shaders/outline_material.tres`.
- Input actions are defined in `project.godot`: `move_*`, `strafe_*`, `target_next`, `ability_1` to `ability_4`, `interact`, `pause`.
- Combat is WoW-style tab-targeting. Physics layers: 1 is world, 2 is units, 3 is corpses.
- World sounds (hits, swings, spells) go through `Sfx.play_at(name, position)`: full volume within 4 yards of the player, fading (`Sfx.falloff`) to silence at 40. Interface sounds use `Sfx.play`. The base level is `Sfx.VOLUME_DB`. Floating combat text is only the player's own fight: hits dealt and taken, misses either way, heals on the player, stuns on the player or their target (`CombatHud._involves_player`).
- World units are yards. Movement follows classic: run 7, backpedal 4.5, walk 2.5, turn 180°/s, no acceleration or air control. Other keys: Space jump, Num Lock autorun, Numpad / walk, / opens the chat with a slash typed (reserved, like Esc and `), Enter opens the chat, B bags, C character.
- Items live in `GameState` (equipment, bags, containers); `scripts/items/inventory.gd` holds the classic bag and equip rules. Stats from gear flow into `CharacterStats` via `GameState.inventory_changed`.
- Start: `scenes/front_end/front_end.tscn` is the main scene: a pretend login (any account and password), character select and creation (classic race/class pairs, starting gear and proficiencies from `data/classic/characters.json`; only Men-at-Arms, Templars and Clerics have spells). Enter World goes through `ui/loading_screen.gd` (a landscape, a tip and a progress bar; it loads on a thread, then fades out over the new world). Esc in game opens `ui/game_menu.gd` (Log Out returns to character select). Running `main.tscn` directly plays the first character.
- Saving: `GameState` saves each character (name, items, `player_state` with position, facing, health, mana) to `user://characters/<name>.json`, with a format `SAVE_VERSION`; login settings are in `user://settings.cfg`. `main.gd` saves every 30 s, after inventory changes, on level-up, death, logging out and quitting. Shift+F12 starts the current character over at level 1. Tests must pass `-- --save=<scratch file>` (one character, skips the front end) or `-- --profile=<scratch dir>` (front end with its own characters and settings) so they never touch the real saves.
- Scratch test scripts run with `-s` load before autoloads, so they must load classes that touch `GameState` (Inventory, ItemDB, LootTable) with `load()` at runtime rather than by class name.
- Dev console: ` (the key under Esc) opens `ui/dev_console.gd` in game, with commands like `additem`, `money`, `level`, `learn`, `trainall` (every spell the class trainer teaches at your level, free), `reset confirm` (start the character over, as Shift+F12), `bis` (the best gear in the game for your class and level, put on where it's an upgrade, and the same for every bot in your party by its own build, what they take off going in their `BotBag`: `scripts/items/best_gear.gd` scores items by the build's stat weights, armor and weapon damage, the build being the talent tree with the most points (`BestGear.SPECS`), whose weapon style wins when a set of it exists: Blademastery and Zeal two-handers, Ferocity two weapons, Bulwark and Aegis a shield, Grace, Devotion and Mercy an off-hand item or shield; also a "BiS" button on the Options window's Developer tab), and the Developer tab has a "Spawn Enemy" row (level, rank, Spawn) for `spawn`, `tele`, `god`, `speed` and `spawn [level] [normal | elite | boss]` (a random creature of the zone's data straight ahead, 8 yards past its aggro range, standing still: at a set level or rank it takes its class's base stats for the level from `creature_class_stats.json`, CMaNGOS creature_template_classlevelstats with multipliers 1, times `Enemy.RANK_HEALTH`/`RANK_DAMAGE` for elites and bosses, which are bigger; bosses show "??" for their level, elites a "+"; `Enemy.temporary` makes it unsaved and gone for good 5 minutes after dying) (`help` lists them). Add a command as a `COMMANDS` entry plus a `_cmd_<name>` method. `bots`, `invite`, `uninvite`, `disband` and `summon` handle playerbots. While it is open `Player.typing` stops the movement keys. `reload` saves, restarts the game with the same arguments plus `-- --resume=<character>` (which skips the login and enters the world) and quits, picking up any change on disk. The Options window's Developer tab runs the same commands (`DevConsole.run`) from buttons, and can turn the ` key off.
- `main.tscn` uses the WoW-style `scenes/camera/orbit_camera.tscn`, which gives the player tank controls. The fixed `game_camera.tscn` is kept for comparison and gives camera-relative movement.
