# AI RPG

3D pixel art action RPG built with Godot 4.6 (Forward+, Jolt physics).

## Running
- Editor/game: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64.exe`
- Console build, for logs and scripted checks: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64_console.exe`
- Run the game: `<console exe> --path C:/ai-rpg`
- Re-import after adding files: `<console exe> --headless --path C:/ai-rpg --import`

## Layout
- `scenes/<area>/`: scenes with their scripts beside them (`main`, `player`, `enemies`, `levels`, `camera`)
- `scripts/combat/`: shared combat logic (`Combatant`, `AbilityCaster`, `Targeting`, `Ability`)
- `data/abilities/`: one `Ability` resource per ability
- `scripts/stats/`, `scripts/items/`, `data/classic/`: classic WoW 1.12 stats, combat table, XP, creatures, items and loot, with exact tables and formulas from the CMaNGOS and vmangos server code (see `data/classic/SOURCE.md`). Keep new rules faithful to classic and note the source. `tools/classic_db/extract.py` regenerates the JSON from the CMaNGOS classic database.
- Spells: `SpellDB` reads `data/classic/spells.json`; `SpellCaster` (on the Player) casts with classic checks, GCD, cooldowns and cast times, and holds each spell's effect code following CMaNGOS. Buffs are `Combatant.auras`. `GameState.known_spells` and `action_bar` are saved. UI: `ui/spell_ui.gd` (action bar 1-0,-,=; spellbook P, laid out as classic's: pages of twelve spells in two columns, with tabs down its right edge for General and each talent tree, a spell's tab coming from its skill line (client SkillLineAbility, `skill_lines.json` from `fetch_client_tables.py`, exported as `book_tabs`, `SpellDB.book_tab`), and a "Hide old ranks" box showing only each spell's highest known rank, saved as the `spellbook_hide_old_ranks` option); buff bar). The target frame shows the target's buffs and debuffs underneath (debuffs are what the other side put on it; the player's own framed gold; hover for what they do), with its cast bar below them. Tooltips are as wide as their widest line (long single-column lines wrap at `ItemTooltip.WIDTH`), never catch the mouse, and sit left of the cursor where there's no room to the right, and above it where there's none below. Four extra action bars can be turned on under Interface (`bar_bottom_left`, `bar_bottom_right`, `bar_right`, `bar_right_2`; `GameState.EXTRA_BARS`): the bottom two stack above the XP bar (the stance and cast bars move up), the right two are columns under the minimap (slots shrink to fit, and the quest tracker and bags move left). Their slots follow the main bar's in `GameState.action_bar` (60 in all, older saves padded), and their buttons are key binding actions `bottom_left_1`... (unbound by default, added to the InputMap by `Settings`); `GameState.slot_action`/`shown_slots` map slots to actions and to the bars shown. New spells go in the first empty slot of a shown bar. Spells are learned from an `Npc` with a `trainer` via `ui/trainer_window.gd` (Train, or Train All for everything available while the money lasts): Sir Aldric Fenn trains Templars ("paladin"), Sister Maud Ellery Clerics ("priest"), Sergeant Bram Holloway (by the smithy) Men-at-Arms ("warrior"); a trainer only teaches its own class (`SpellDB.TRAINER_CLASSES`, which also says which classes have spells). Godric Vane (the Weapon Master, by the smithy) teaches every class the weapon skills it may learn, 10 silver each (Polearms 1 gold from level 20): classic's weapon masters' lists joined (the `weapons` trainer, `WEAPON_MASTERS` in `extract.py`; each entry's `classes` mask from SkillRaceClassInfo, `weapon_skills.json`, which `SpellDB.trainer_list` filters by). Learning any proficiency spell grants its item subclasses (`SpellDB.proficiencies_of`), skills you started with show as known, and weapon skills stay out of the spellbook (`SpellDB.is_weapon_skill`). Bots know every weapon skill their class may learn at their level (`Bot.learn_spells`), so a Blademastery build's BiS is a two-hander. The Cleric list merges classic's five racial priest trainers (`CLASS_TRAINERS` in `extract.py`); racial spells carry `races` and `SpellDB.trainer_list(trainer, race)` leaves out other races'. Every Man-at-Arms, Templar and Cleric spell works except those needing another player (`NEEDS_PARTY`) or a missing system (`NOT_YET`: Dominate Will, Borrowed Eyes, Font of Light); racial first ranks and class-quest spells (Bastion and Fury Stance, Goad, Revival's first rank) are sold free (`RACIAL_FIRST_RANKS`, `QUEST_SPELLS` in `extract.py`); abilities that are talents come from the talent (the trainer sells their later ranks). The Man-at-Arms (classic Warrior) is in `scripts/spells/warrior_abilities.gd`, a child of the SpellCaster: rage (`Combatant.power_type`; rage spells' costs are tenths; earned from white hits dealt and damage taken by CMaNGOS's RewardRage formula, fading 3 a tick out of combat, never refilled like mana), stances (shapeshift auras on the stance bar, each with its passive via `SPELL_LINKS`; changing stance loses rage; new Men-at-Arms start in Vanguard Stance; abilities need the stances in their `Stances` mask), next-swing attacks (`Combatant.swing_replacer`), Rush and Cut Off, reactive strikes (Riposte Strike after a dodge, Retort after you avoid), shields, area attacks, shouts and taunts (`Enemy.taunt`, `AURA_MOD_TAUNT`). Two-Weapon Fighting lets one-handers go in the off hand, which swings on its own timer for half damage with classic's dual-wield miss. Spells another spell casts that the data doesn't link (Blessed Burst's heal, Withering Touch's and Umbral Guard's strikes) are `SPELL_LINKS` there (`SpellDB.linked_spell`). `SpellCaster` handles effects by kind (heals, damage of any school, buffs, debuffs with a hit roll, channels that moving or another spell ends, absorbs, fear that makes creatures flee, dispels) with the odd ones by name; the matching aura types (stats, dodge, speed, threat, aggro range, damage and healing percentages, heal/leech/trigger ticks) are read in `Combatant`, `CharacterStats` and `Enemy`. Combat-driven effects (Oath procs at classic procs-per-minute, Verdict debuffs that heal or restore, damage shields, hallowed ground, Umbral Guard and Withering Touch striking back, Inner Flame charges, Leeching Embrace) are in `scripts/spells/spell_procs.gd`; periodic aura ticks in `Combatant`. New ranks replace older ranks on the action bar (unlike classic). Class buffs lasting 5 minutes or more in classic (Boons and Greater Boons, Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit, Inner Flame, Shadow Warding, Zealous Fervor...) last an hour (`SpellDB._lengthen_buffs`, unlike classic; short ones such as Boon of Shelter and Rallying Cry keep theirs). "Boons" and "Greater Boons" are flyouts (`SpellDB.FLYOUTS`, ids no classic spell uses): spellbook entries that lead their tab and go on the action bar like spells, and clicking one (or its key, `Player.flyout_pressed`) opens a row of the boons you know, highest ranks, to cast one (`SpellUI._toggle_flyout`; upward from bottom bars, leftward from right ones, right-click in the spellbook). Templar Presences (classic paladin auras) get their own stance bar above the action bar, filled automatically in the order first learned. Stances, forms and Presences also show in the buff bar like any buff; never hide them there (only passive spells' auras, such as a stance's "Stance Passive" part, are left out). The stance bar frames the active one in gold. Tooltip descriptions for spells (and item Use:/Equip: lines) come from `scripts/spells/spell_text.gd`, written in the game's own words with numbers from the spell data; give a new spell a line there.
- Spellsword: the game's own class (ClassicData.CharClass.SPELLSWORD = 10, no classic class has the number), designed in `docs/class_designs/spellsword.json` (its page in the README there). Its data is generated: `tools/class_designs/build_class_data.py` turns the design (abilities with their ranks and text, talents with their values) and a spec of how each works into `data/classes/spellsword.json`, in spells.json's shape (spell_template rows from id 900000 in its own spell family 12, one SpellFamilyFlags bit per ability so talents work as spell modifiers; chains, the `spellsword` trainer list with Templar costs by level and Wardweave Form free at 10 as a class-quest spell, Mail at 40; start spells; talents["10"]; book tabs; form passives as links), plus its player tables (each race's Man-at-Arms stats plus the Human Templar's difference from them; the Templar's health and mana) and starting kits (a two-handed sword of its own, the Initiate's Greatsword, in the file's `items`, which ItemDB merges; and the race's Man-at-Arms clothes). Rows carry `Description` (SpellText uses it) and `Icon` (Icons.spell_key uses it). `ClassicData.CLASS_FILES` lists the game's own class files: `ClassicData` merges their tables and kits, `SpellDB` their spell data, so `extract.py` can regenerate the classic data untouched. Rerun the generator after changing the design. Races: Human, Wood Elf, Undead, Gnome, Troll. Its abilities are `scripts/spells/spellsword_abilities.gd` (`SpellswordAbilities`, a child of the SpellCaster beside WarriorAbilities, asked first in `_find_problem`/`_finish`): forms Bladesong (20: +10% damage) and Wardweave (21: 45% more threat, 10% less damage taken and dealt, stronger shields, and 2 armor a point of Intellect read in CharacterStats) on the stance bar; Edges (Flame, Frost, Storm, Warding), weapon imbues whose school damage and effect ride every melee hit (`Combatant.proc_event`), scaled by Bladecraft (a quarter of attack power); Resonance (`Combatant.resonance`, 0-5, built by Edged hits, faded 10 s after combat, shown as five gems on the player frame) spent by Release as the Edge inscribed last says (Flame Burst, Rime Lance, Thunderclap, Bulwark of Runes); the rest by name (Phase Step, Binding Glyph, Mirror Guard reflecting a creature's spell through `Combatant.damage_filter`, Glyph Storm's channel, Bastion of Glyphs through `Combatant.guardian`...). Talents with rules of their own are dummy auras read by name (`_talent`); two aura types of our own (`SpellDB.AURA_CONTROL_DURATION_PCT`, `AURA_CRIT_DAMAGE_TAKEN_PCT`) are read by Combatant. Trainers: Magister Ilsabet Rhee in the priory, Tamsin Vey in Wexford. Bots (`Bot._use_spellsword_spells`) tank in Wardweave with Warding Edge when they hold a shield in a party or are told (the party bar's Form), else fight in Bladesong with Flame or Storm Edge; the party bar's categories are Edge and Form.
- Ranger: classic's Hunter (class 3; Dwarf, Wood Elf, Orc, Troll and Hornkin), its spells from Holt Thunderhorn's list (`CLASS_TRAINERS["hunter"]`), with the game's names (Spirits for Aspects, Quarry's Mark, Glimmer Shot, Talon Strike, Still as Death, Mend Companion...). Its abilities are `scripts/spells/ranger_abilities.gd` (`RangerAbilities`, a child of the SpellCaster beside the other classes'): Auto Shot (75) is Shoot on its own button; shots (DmgClass 3) roll the ranged table (`CombatRules.roll_ranged`, ranged crit apart from melee: `Combatant.ranged_crit`), normalised weapon damage plus ranged attack power, no ammo (the weapon alone); stings and Hobbling Shot as debuffs; Spirits on the stance bar like Presences (`SpellDB.is_aspect`; Cheetah and Pack Dazed by a hit); traps (`scenes/objects/trap.gd`, `Trap`: Summon Object effects, `spells.json` "traps", arming after 2 s, firing on the first enemy near, sharing a cooldown); Still as Death; Arrow Rain's channel at the enemy target; tracking dots on the minimap (`tracked_type`); Weasel Bite after a dodge, Counterattack after a parry; Distant Gaze through `OrbitCamera.far_sight`. Pets (classic's in full but happiness and loyalty): `scripts/pets/pet_db.gd` (`PetDB`, `data/classic/pets.json` from pet_levelstats, the families from the client's CreatureFamily and SkillLineAbility via `fetch_client_tables.py pets`, `pet_families.json`; tameable from CreatureTypeFlags, a wild beast's abilities from PetSpellDataId), `scenes/pets/pet.gd` (`Pet`: stats by level and family, focus, Follow/Stay/Attack, Aggressive/Defensive/Passive, autocast abilities through `scripts/pets/pet_abilities.gd`, its owner's kill XP up to its owner's level, ticked far with a far bot), and the hidden passives every pet has (`spells.json` "pet_passives") whose amounts are its owner's pet talents (`Combatant.add_passive_aura(spell, modified_by)`). Tame Beast is a 20 s channel on a tameable beast of your level or below; Call, Dismiss, Revive and Mend Companion; the record is `GameState.pet` (saved; a bot's `Bot.pet_record`, in its roster entry), `GameState.stable` with two bought slots (`ui/stable_window.gd`, at a stable master: an `Npc` with `stable`), and `GameState.pet_spells`, the abilities the Ranger can teach, learned by taming a beast that knows them or from a pet trainer (the `pet` trainer list, its early-patch passives left out), taught in `ui/beast_training_window.gd` for 5 training points a pet level (classic's best loyalty; costs are a guess, see TODO). UI: the pet frame under the player frame (abandon from its menu), the pet bar above the action bar (commands, four abilities with right-click autocast, stances), a strip under a party Ranger's frame. Taming quests at 10 from each race's Ranger trainer in its second zone (taming rods, `QuestSpells.TAMING_RODS`), then Training the Beast at the pet trainer, which teaches the pet spells (`quests.json` "learns"). Talents (Beastcraft, Sharpshooting, Woodcraft): most are spell modifiers (1.12's modifier 12 is speed, `SpellMods.SPEED`: Trailblazing); ranged-weapon talents count only for ranged attacks (`Combatant.ranged_only`, the ranged slot in `Talents._requirement_met`); Monster Hunter and Manhunter by creature type (`creature_type_mask`, auras 168/169); and written out: Quick Shots from Hawk Spirit, Frenzy, Kindred Bond, Menace, Beast Fury (stun immunity, `is_immune_to_mechanic`), Drake Sting's poison when the sleep ends, Improved Mend Companion, Ensnaring Traps, Hardy (aura 133). Trainers in every start of its races and their second zones; pet trainers and stable masters in Kettlefold, Fernwatch, Spikewall and Longhorn Village. Bots (`Bot._use_ranger_spells`) know the pet spells from 10 and tame the nearest beast they can (`_tame_step`, skipping the quests), keep a Spirit (the party bar's Spirits category), send the pet in, shoot from 25 yards with Quarry's Mark, Adder Sting, Glimmer Shot and Spread Shot, fight in the dead zone with Talon Strike, Crippling Cut and the reactive strikes, and lie Still as Death when losing.
- Talents: classic's trees for the classes with spells, from the client's Talent and TalentTab tables (`fetch_client_tables.py` writes `talents.json`; `extract.py` exports every rank's spell and renames the trees: Man-at-Arms Blademastery, Ferocity, Bulwark; Templar Grace, Aegis, Zeal; Ranger Beastcraft, Sharpshooting, Woodcraft; Cleric Devotion, Mercy, Umbra). `scripts/spells/talents.gd` has the rules: a point a level from 10, 5 points in a tree per tier, prerequisites; ranks are `CharacterProfile.talents` (saved; `Talents.validate` on load), unlearning at the class trainer costs 1g, 5g, 10g, then 5g more each time to 50g (`talent_reset_cost`, saved), and the `resettalents` command is free. Talent abilities join the known spells; passive talents become permanent auras (`Combatant.add_passive_aura`, `aura.talent`), their highest rank only, while their weapon, shield or stance requirement holds (`Talents.sync_passives`, run by `CharacterStats.refresh`). Talents that change other spells are spell modifiers (`scripts/spells/spell_mods.gd`, CMaNGOS `ApplySpellMod`: `SpellDB.effect_value`/`cooldown`/`duration`/`cast_time`/`spell_range`/`effect_radius`/`mana_cost` take an optional caster; crit, damage, pushback, resist, crit bonus and charges are applied where those happen); a modifier with no mask of its own takes `spell_affect`'s, and later ranks take the first rank's. Combat events are `Combatant.proc_event` (CMaNGOS ProcFlags and procEx, with the spell); `scripts/spells/talent_procs.gd` fires talents' triggered spells from them (the first rank's `spell_proc_event` row covers every rank), with Lingering Wounds, Inner Light, Hallowed Recovery, Tooth for a Tooth and Gloomweave written out, and uses up talent buffs' charges. Stat talents are read in `CharacterStats`, `Combatant` and `CombatRules`. Descriptions are worked out in `scripts/spells/talent_text.gd`. The window is `ui/talent_window.gd` (N, the `talents` action). Bots spend their points down a main tree (`Talents.bot_trees`, `spend_for_bot`), skipping `BOT_SKIPS` (Lingering Spirit and Font of Light aren't in the game yet; the Ranger's talents that do nothing here).
- The game's own items: `data/items.json` (`ItemDB.own_items`, merged into the item data; its `vendors` add them to vendors' classic stock, `VendorWindow.vendor_data`). Classic has no helm below level 25 except the Red Defias Mask, so Harrowfield's level 8-10 brigands drop that (Red Brigand Mask, about 2% as classic's Defias of 10 and up; `EXTRA_LOOT` in `extract.py`), and the file adds a white helm for cloth, leather and mail at level 8 (Woolen Cap and Tanned Leather Cap from Piers Tanner in Wexford, Ringmail Coif from Hugh Stannard in the priory); no plate, as classic gives plate only from 40. Miscellaneous head items (masks, hats) show as a hood.
- NPCs: `scenes/npcs/npc.tscn` with `trainer` and/or `vendor` (a creature entry in `data/classic/vendors.json`), and a `race` (the model's: Humans unless set, Kareth's orcs and trolls; `appearance` sets the body). Vendors buy, sell (right-click bag items) and buy back, following CMaNGOS. Food and drink are item "Use:" spells handled by `SpellCaster.use_item`; X sits.
- Names are the game's own, not classic's: `tools/classic_db/renames.py` maps every classic item, creature, NPC and spell name to an original one when the data is exported (plain words like Wolf and Human stay). Class names are the game's own too (`ClassicData.CLASS_NAMES`: Man-at-Arms, Templar, Ranger, Cutpurse, Cleric, Seer, Magus, Hexer, Wildwarden, and the game's own Spellsword); code and data keep classic's names for the enum and the "paladin" trainer. Code that spots spell kinds by name uses the new patterns: `Oath of` seals, `Verdict` judgements, `Boon of` blessings, `... Presence` auras.
- World: two continents (`docs/world/`), each a world of its own: `scenes/levels/world.tscn` (`World`, with the sky and sun) builds only the zones of the continent the character is on (`World.CONTINENTS`, zone name to scene, a zone whose scene isn't there yet left out; `World.continent`, from `GameState.player_state.continent`, a new character's race's start, else Aldmark for older saves; `World.continent_for`), and of those only the ones joined by passes to the zone the character is in (`World.LINKS`, `region_of`, `zone_for`: the save's zone, its graveyard, else the race's start), since a continent's halves aren't joined until the zones between are built, side by side, in world coordinates, so bots walk between them; the two worlds' coordinates overlap, so each zone has a `continent` and `Zone.PASSES` is per continent. Every race starts at its `World.START_ZONES` zone (`start_zone`, Ashford Vale if that zone isn't built): Humans in Ashford Vale, Dwarves and Gnomes in Frosthollow in Aldmark's mountains, Wood Elves in Mossglen in Kareth's north-west, Orcs and Trolls in Emberhollow on Kareth, the Undead in Gravesend in Aldmark's north and Hornkin at Sunfold Camp in Kareth's west; `start_zone_here` is it on this continent (the Homing Token, `tele spawn`). `World.zone_at(point)` says which zone a point is in (the one whose square holds it), and `height_at`, `places()`, `spawns()` and `enclosures()` answer for all of them. Each zone is a `Zone` (`scenes/levels/zone.gd`: its name, center, size, graveyard, hub, places, spawns and enclosures, plus shared ground, water, edge walls, clutter, torch, plank and creature-spawning helpers), built in code from the layout constants in its script, its NPCs in its `.tscn`. Zones have mountains along their edges from the same noise (`Zone.base_height`, `mountain_height`), so where two meet the ground joins exactly; `Zone.PASSES` are lines cut through the mountains on both sides (`carve_passes`, highest where they cross the border named by `x` or `z`) where the edge walls open. `scenes/levels/ashford_vale/` is the starting area (levels 1-6, centered on the origin): terrain from a height function (`scenes/levels/terrain.gd`, built from arrays with heights and vertex colors worked out a row at a time on worker threads (`Terrain.parallel`; the `AI_RPG_SERIAL` environment variable runs them on one thread, for chasing crashes), so zones' height and color functions must only read; tree and rock bodies are added to the world after their shapes, as adding shapes to a body in the world rebuilds it each time), stream, roads, the priory, mine, brigand camp, bridge, seeded trees and rocks (`scenes/levels/props.gd`), and creature spawn groups. `scenes/levels/harrowfield/` (levels 6-12) is farmland over the pass south of the vale's bridge, much bigger than the starting vale as classic's second zones are: 1000 yards across (x -500 to 500, z 200 to 1200, so its north edge meets the vale's south edge), with mountains as deep as the vale's at any size (`MOUNTAIN_START`), ground every 2.5 yards, and its places laid out round named flats (`FLATS` with index constants) that the builders place props relative to. Its places: the village of Wexford (inn, chapel and churchyard, Marshal Edric Thorne), the Coombe farm with its fields and pumpkin patch, the Wainwright cottage, the Old Delve mine (northwest hills) and Copperhollow (a second burrowling mine, northeast hills), the Mirrow Mere, Thornwick Tower (hedge-mages), the Ashwood lumber camp in the east woods, the Pellow vineyard, a brigand farmstead, the south watch and bridge, the Reedford landing on the stream, the west garrison, the mirefin village on the mere's east shore, and the gnoll hills with Grunmaw's camp. Wexford has a Templar trainer and a Cleric trainer before the chapel and, at the smithy, a Man-at-Arms trainer and Hesketh Steele the weaponsmith (classic Corina Steele's list, from `npc_vendor_template`; vendors with a `VendorTemplateId` export that list after their own, as CMaNGOS sells both); Foreman Edda Harlow keeps the lumber camp; its creatures and quests are classic Elwynn Forest's. Kareth's are `scenes/levels/emberhollow/` (levels 1-6, classic's Valley of Trials, a 400-yard red canyon at (2500, -1700): the Proving Ground, a palisaded orc hold with its gate west, round huts, a war drum and a tent smithy; boars on the flats, sand scorpions on the Scorpion Rocks, the Ember Cult's cave in the northeastern hill with its imps and ashstalkers, Rustpincer on his ridge, cactus groves with Cactus Apples; Man-at-Arms, Cleric and Spellsword trainers, a provisioner, armourer and clothier) and `scenes/levels/redsand_coast/` (levels 6-12, classic's Durotar, 1000 yards at (1800, -1900), west of Emberhollow over the pass: Spikewall, the Horde's palisade town, with every trainer, the Weapon Master Grakkar, the inn and vendors; the sea in a bay along the south (`COAST`, wadeable `SEA_BED`), the Tidebone trolls' stilt village on its western shore and Vazzar's Tidebone Isles offshore, the Saltmarch humans' Stranded Keep and their wreck on the eastern shore, the Redwater with its bridge, the tuskfolk's dry gulch and the Dunestride centaurs in the west, the harpies' cliffs, Stormcrag Ridge with an Ember Cult camp, the tigers' hills, Skull Rock and the shut gate of Ironmaw Hold in the northeast); `Zone.palisade` and `Zone.round_hut` build the Horde's walls and huts. Aldmark's north: `scenes/levels/gravesend/` (levels 1-6, classic's Deathknell, 400 yards at (-2500, -4200): the ruined Gravesend Chapel and its graveyard where the Undead rise, zombies in the old field, Rattlebone skeletons at the old crypts, Duskweb spiders in the Webbed Vault, the Sunward Crusade's camp, Equipment Boxes in the ruined hamlet) and `scenes/levels/duskmoor/` (`DuskmoorGlades`, 6-12, Tirisfal Glades, 1200 yards at (-1700, -4200): Gallowfield, the Ashworth mills, manor and crypt, Lake Hollowmere and its Murkfin, the Plaguewood farms' pumpkins, the Brannick farm, the Grimhide gnolls, the east barricade, and the Sunward Crusade's watch, mission, east watch and Zealot Abbey). Bots keep to zones they can walk to (`World.reachable`, `BotManager.hunting_spot`'s `near`, quest givers and chests), new ones spawn in the player's region, and a bot gives up quests whose ender it can't reach (`Bot._drop_unreachable_quests`). Saves store the continent and zone; the dead come back at the graveyard of the zone they died in (`Zone.graveyard`: the priory, Wexford's churchyard, the Proving Ground, Spikewall, the Hollow Hall, Kettlefold, the Elderbough, Fernwatch...), and anything else starts at its race's start. Enemy node names come from each zone's spawn layout (with a prefix per zone), so changing `SPAWNS` orphans saved corpses (harmless). `tele <zone>` goes to a zone's graveyard; a zone on the other continent saves the player at its graveyard and restarts the game there (`DevConsole._change_continent`).
- World plan: `docs/world/` plans the two continents, Aldmark and Kareth, levels 1-60: each zone stands in for one classic zone (its creatures, loot and quests), with the game's own name, place in world yards (`rect`, as `World` has it; Ashford Vale and Harrowfield where they are), settlement, places, links (roads and passes), dungeons, and the ships, airships and tram between them. `tools/world/plan_world.py` holds it, writes `docs/world/zones.json` and the README's tables, and checks that zones don't overlap and links join zones sharing an edge. Build new zones from it.
- Quests: `QuestDB` joins each quest's classic rules (`data/classic/quests.json`, exported from quest_template for the ids in `extract.py`'s `QUESTS`) with the game's own title, text, giver and ender (`data/quests.json`; givers are NPC node names). `QuestLog` has the rules (taking, kill credit, quest-only drops, turning in, classic quest XP; a negative PrevQuestId is a quest to have underway, as CMaNGOS has it, `can_take` and `BotQuests.can_take`; a quest's `races` is classic's RequiredRaces, `QuestDB.race_allowed`, so each faction and race takes its own); progress is `GameState.quests` and `quests_done`, saved. UI: `ui/quest_window.gd` (talking to an NPC with quests), `ui/quest_log_ui.gd` (L), the tracker and the "!"/"?" markers in `CombatHud`. To add a quest, add its classic entry to `QUESTS`, re-export, and write its text in `data/quests.json`. Exploration quests (QuestFlags 4, classic's area triggers) name a place of the zones in their text's `explore` ("the Old Delve", "Copperhollow"): walking into it (`QuestLog.check_exploration`, from `main.gd`) says "... explored.", and bots walk there (`BotQuests.place_to_explore`, `Bot._check_explored`). Quests with things to use follow CMaNGOS: quest items' spells are run by `scripts/quests/quest_spells.gd` (`QuestSpells`, from `SpellCaster.use_item`, its cast like any item spell's): Create Item near a spell focus object (`RequiresSpellFocus`; zones list their focus points in `spell_focuses`, `World.spell_focus_near`: Crown of the Earth's phials at the moonwells), Quest Complete (the Tallgrass's wells cleansed with totems; Release Wildclaw on his body), spells a quest counts on a creature or object (`objects` in quests.json, `[target, count, spell]`, a negative target an object, from ReqCreatureOrGO and ReqSpellCast, `QuestLog.credit_object`: Samuel's Remains buried in Maren's Grave), and dummies scripted in `DUMMY_TARGETS` (Awaken Peon on a sleeping Lazy Peon, an `Npc` with `sleeping` and `creature_entry`). Objects used by hand are `GameObject`s of type 10 (the Dunestride attack plans; `open` counts them). Quests finished by an event (SpecialFlags 2, `QuestLog.complete_event`, the text's `event` line), repeatable ones (SpecialFlags 1: Distracting Jarvik, after which Highpeak swaps the guarded Thunder Ale barrel for the unguarded one, `QuestObject.set_present`), timed ones (LimitTime: `time_left`, counted by `QuestLog.tick`, failing them as classic, shown in the tracker), escorts (an `Npc` with `escort`: Mist follows the player, finishing at the quest's ender, failing if left 60 yards behind or the player dies) and source items (ReqSourceId, `sources`: quest drops while needed) are all in `QuestLog`; a text's `objective_text` renames a kill line ("Lazy Peons woken"). Bots leave such quests alone (`QuestDB.bots_can_do`). Items with a classic `startquest` (the Coin Collection Ledger) offer their quest when used from the bags ("This Item Begins a Quest" on their tooltip; `QuestWindow.open_item`), and accepting takes the item unless the quest needs it; bots don't start these. Game objects (`data/classic/gameobjects.json`, from `GAMEOBJECTS` in `extract.py`; zones place them by `quest_objects` and `chests`, `Zone.spawn_objects`): quest givers and enders that are things are `QuestObject`s (`scenes/objects/quest_object.gd`, an `Npc` with a body's look, its quest marker and no nameplate; A half-eaten body, Aldous's corpse), named by node like any NPC; chests are `GameObject`s (`scenes/objects/game_object.gd`, on the corpse layer: right-click opens them like a corpse in the loot window, `LootTable.roll_object`), which sparkle and open only while they hold something a quest underway needs, and are gone until their classic respawn time once emptied; bots fetch from them (`Bot._fetch_from_chest`).
- Enemies are set by `creature_entry` (a classic creature_template id); their stats, loot, money, respawn time (half classic's, `Enemy.RESPAWN_SCALE`) and wander distance come from the data. Their size and colour are in `Enemy.LOOKS` (the capsule's for creatures without a model). Whether they attack on sight comes from their classic faction (`data/classic/factions.json`, rules in `ClassicData`): neutral ones and hostile ones flagged no-aggro only fight back, and all passive creatures show yellow. A zone can make creatures passive regardless (`Enemy.passive`; Ashford Vale's `PASSIVE_CREATURES` covers every burrowling). Creatures cast their classic spells from their EventAI script (`creature_ai_scripts`, exported as each creature's `Events`; only cast and ranged-mode actions): `scenes/enemies/creature_ai.gd` (`Enemy.ai`, made for creatures with events) runs the timed, aggro, spawn, range, missing-buff and behind-the-target events, casts with cast times, mana (or a refilling 100 energy), cooldowns, pushback and missile travel, scales damage and mana cost to the creature's level (`creature_class_damage.json`, from `creature_template_classlevelstats`), and is interrupted by stuns, fear, silence (`PreventionType`) and interrupt effects (`Combatant.interrupt_cast`, which locks the school; Hilt Strike and Shield Smash call it). Casters in ranged mode stand off and cast their main spell, closing to melee when out of mana, silenced or locked out. The target frame shows the target's cast (`CombatHud.unit_cast`). Ashford Vale's casters: Burrowling Firecaller (Flame Bolt, Rime Shroud), Pickman (Prying Strike), Brigand Footpad (Back Thrust from behind), Ridgetusk Boar (Headlong Charge); Harrowfield adds the Brigand Hedge-Mage (Rime Bolt, Rime Shroud), Brigand Cutthroat (Snap Kick), Mirefin Lurker (Back Thrust), Mirefin Forager (a potion at low health), the spiders' poison, the Ditchjaw Outrider's Thrash and Grunmaw (Prying Strike, Head Butt), and gnolls and Brasstooth flee at low health. Creature auras that fire on their own melee hits (ProcFlags done melee: Poison Proc, Thrash) are `CreatureAI._on_proc_event`; ones that strike back at attackers are `_on_melee_hit_taken`. To add one, put its creature_template id in `extract.py`'s `CREATURES` and name it and its spells in `renames.py`. Pulling a creature calls for help (CMaNGOS `MarkCallAssistanceOnPull`): creatures of its faction within its `CallForHelp` radius (5 yards by default) that see it, aren't fighting and attack on sight join 2 s later, and their pulls can bring more (`Enemy._mark_assistance`); passive and neutral creatures never come. In Ashford Vale only the brigands do. EventAI can also make a creature flee in a panic for 10 s at low health (`Enemy.flee`, "... tries to run away in fear!") or call for help in a radius (`Enemy.call_for_help`); no vale creature has those in the data. Creatures the data has speak (on aggro, mostly) say lines of the game's own (`CreatureAI.SAYINGS`), in the chat as creature speech (`Chat.monster`, the "Creature Speech" filter).
- Playerbots: computer-run player characters (`scenes/bots/bot.gd`, made by `BotManager` from the Playerbots options). The player and bots are both `PlayerUnit`s, and each has a `CharacterProfile` (name, race, class, level, XP, gear, known spells; `GameState` extends it for the player), which `CharacterStats`, `Progression` and `SpellCaster` read, so bots follow the same rules and cast the same spells (`PlayerUnit.enemy_target()`/`friendly_target()` pick the targets). On their own, bots do quests (the "Random Bots Do Quests" option): `BotQuests` (`scenes/bots/bot_quests.gd`) is a bot's own quest log with the player's classic rules, up to three at a time, saved in its roster entry; bots walk to givers to take and hand in quests (quest XP for their level), hunt the creatures their quests need first, count their own kills, and roll each creature's classic drop chance for quest items (`LootTable.drop_chance`), since they don't loot. Random bots (not in the party) leave what the player's quests underway still need to the player: they don't start on creatures to kill or that drop a wanted quest item, and don't open chests holding one (`BotManager.player_needs`, `Bot._can_hunt`, `_nearest_chest`), though they finish a fight already begun and fight back; party bots help with them. Otherwise they hunt creatures near their level, eat and drink when low, and get up 30 s after dying (at their zone's graveyard when on their own). They go in and out of walled places by the gate (the zones' enclosures) and between zones by the pass, joining it where it's nearest (`BotManager.route`, worked out again when a bot is moved off its way, as when raised at a graveyard); a bot that stops getting anywhere takes a detour round whatever is in the way (`scenes/bots/bot_paths.gd`, `BotPaths`, `BotManager.paths`: an AStarGrid2D search over yard cells near it, each found open or blocked once by a physics test of a bot-sized box above the ground and kept, straightened into waypoints; at most two fresh searches a physics frame), hopping only when there's none, and a far bot (which walks through walls) that comes near the player standing in something steps out to the nearest open cell (`BotPaths.free_spot`); quests, quest givers and hunting grounds in either zone draw them across, so bots past the vale's levels head for Harrowfield. Far bots' long steps stop on each waypoint rather than overshooting it. Delivery quests' items (a letter, a pie) count as had once taken. Bots interrupt casting creatures (Hilt Strike, Shield Smash, Hush). Templar bots heal, keep a Presence, an Oath and Boons up, and use Revival. Man-at-Arms bots take a stance (Bastion when tanking, else Vanguard), Rush in, shout Rallying Cry in fights, and use finishers, reactive strikes, Gash, area attacks when crowded and Mighty Blow with spare rage. Cleric bots heal (a Ward first when someone is in trouble, Communal Mending for three hurt), cure diseases, raise the fallen, keep Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit and their own buffs up, and fight from 25 yards with Gnawing Shadow, Mind Lash and Chastise while they have mana. Bots are made only as playable races and classes (`ClassicData.is_playable`); saved bots of others wait in the roster (`BotManager._fits`). The roster is `playerbots.json` in the profile folder, shared by all characters: name, race, class, level, XP, its equipment (put back over its starting gear on spawn, `BotManager._restore_equipment`), and each bot's state (`Bot.saved_state()`: zone, position, facing, health, mana, death, a Stay order, its buffs and item cooldowns, saved as the player's are), saved with the game, so bots carry on where they were. Creatures keep a classic threat table (`Enemy.threat`, heals draw half their amount) and the first player unit to hit one taps it (`tapped_by`); only the player's group gets XP, quest credit and loot from its taps.
- Bot performance: bots and creatures more than 45 yards from the player (`Bot.FAR`; bots not in the party or a trade) have their nodes' own per-frame work switched off (`Bot.set_far`, `Enemy.set_far`) and are ticked by the `BotManager` instead (`far_tick`): in turn, each at most 10 times a second, until 3 ms of the frame are used (`BotManager.far_schedule`, `FAR_BUDGET`), so however many there are they cost about the same frame time, and with more each is ticked less often (a step is capped at half a second, so a crowded world's far side runs slow). Far units walk the ground's height without collision; heights come from the built terrain's own grid (`Terrain.sample` via `Zone.ground_height`, exactly the collision surface). Far bots think every 0.75 s and re-check quest needs and quest givers every 2 s (which creatures drop a quest item is worked out once per item, `BotQuests.droppers`). Creatures look for players to attack 5 times a second, checking only the player units in the 50-yard squares around them (`Enemy._units_near`), and bots look for creatures the same way (`BotManager.creatures_near`), both grids made once a physics frame; creatures standing still skip collision. Physics runs at most 3 steps a frame (`physics/common/max_physics_steps_per_frame`), so an overloaded frame slows the game a little rather than spiralling. Measured on the dev machine with both zones (276 creatures, Harrowfield at its full size): 75 fps (its cap) with none, 200 bots about 72, 250 about 69, 400 about 59, with far units ticked about 8 times a second at 250-400. The Random Bots option goes up to 400.
- Party: the `Party` autoload holds up to four bots with the player (`GameState.party` saves their names). The party bar (`ui/party_bar.gd`, `PartyBar`) is a pet-bar-like bar of icons above the menu buttons (`MicroMenu.top`), shown while you have a party unless hidden with K (the `party_commands` action, its menu button; the `party_bar_shown` option), taking no keys. From the bottom: orders for the member picked or everyone, Follow, Stay, Attack (your target, `Bot.order_attack`), how it fights (click to go through Assist, Defend, Passive: `Bot.behavior`, else the Party Bots Attack option, `Bot.fight_mode`), its role for a member whose class has more than one (Tank, Healer, Damage by `Bot.CLASS_ROLES`; click for the next, right-click for Auto, the class's usual one: Men-at-Arms and Spellswords tank with a shield in a party, Templars and Clerics heal; `Bot.role`, `current_role`, `set_role`, saved: its talents are spent again for the role by `Talents.bot_trees(class, role)`, and it fights by it: tanks take Bastion Stance or Wardweave Form and taunt, a tank Templar keeps Zealous Fervor and uses Hallowed Ground, healers heal below the Bots Heal Below option and others only below 35% (`_heal_below`), a damage Cleric casts from range to its last mana), Sell (to a vendor within 30 yards of you, click junk, right-click everything in its bag: it walks over, `Bot.send_to_vendor`), Train (at its class trainer near you, `Bot.send_to_trainer`, with the count to learn), Bag, Rest; a slot per buff category of the picked member's class showing the spell it keeps up (`Bot.BUFF_CATEGORIES`/`CATEGORY_SPELLS`: Templar Presence, Oath, Boon; Cleric Fortitude, Spirit, Warding, Self; Man-at-Arms Stance, Shout; Auto marked A, None as the category's letter, `Bot.buff_choices`), click for its choices in a column above the bar, right-click for Auto; then the members' portraits and Everyone, to pick whom the orders are for. Hover any icon for what it does. Bots on their own learn their spells as they level; party bots learn them only when trained (`Bot.trained_level`; they say so in party chat on levelling), paying their trainer's classic costs from their own money and then yours (`Bot.train`); leaving the party, they catch up. Behaviour, buff choices and trained level are saved in `Bot.saved_state`. Bag opens `ui/bot_bag_window.gd` (`BotBagWindow`) left of your bags: the bot's equipment and BotBag; right-click its items to take them, right-click your bag items (`InventoryUI.giving`, `give_requested`) or drop the held one there to give them (nothing is bought or sold; soulbound items pass between party members too and stay soulbound in its bag, marked `bound`, which it never sells, trades or offers, `BotBag.is_bound`), click its items to have it put them on, drop your gear on its slots to put it on there, right-click what it wears to take it off; `scripts/items/bot_gear.gd` (`BotGear`) has the equip rules for any profile. Clicking a bot in the world only targets it; right-click the target frame (or a party frame, or its name in chat) for its menu: whisper, invite, trade, or tell a member to follow or stay. Bots keep their own level in the party. With "Teleport to Me When Left Behind" on, a following bot teleports beside you when it's over 40 yards away (even mid-fight) or has gained no ground for 4 s while over 12 yards back; bots told to Stay don't. Quest XP the player earns goes to every party bot too, the same amount (`Party.share_quest_xp`, unlike classic). Kill XP is shared by `Group::RewardGroupAtKill` rules (members alive within 74 yards, by level, with the group bonus), and looted money is split. Party frames are under the player frame, with each member's health, mana and XP; members show on the minimap. The target frame shows a mana bar for targets with mana (creatures have their classic mana).
- Chat: the `Chat` autoload holds the log (kept across the reload after death, not saved) and classic's colors and channels (1. General, 2. Trade, 3. LocalDefense, 4. LookingForGroup; General and LocalDefense are per zone, though the tag doesn't name it); `Chat.say` only reaches the player within say (25 yd) or yell (300 yd) range. `ui/chat_frame.gd` is the box in the bottom-left, with tabs: General (every channel, can't be closed) plus any the player adds with "+"; right-click a tab for New Tab, Edit Tab (name and which kinds of line it shows, `ChatFrame.CATEGORIES`), Close Tab and Lock/Unlock Chat; unlocked, drag a tab to move it and the corner grip to resize it. Tabs, place, size and lock are saved as `chatframe_tabs`, `chatframe_rect` and `chatframe_locked` in the options. Enter to type, Esc to cancel, Up/Down for history, sticky channels, "/p " and the like switch the channel as you type, click a name to whisper, right-click it for the bot's menu (as on its frame). Links: shift-click an item in the bags or character window, a quest in the quest log, or a link in the chat to put "[Name]" in the line; any "[Name]" naming a real item or a quest's title is linked (`Chat.link_text`), items in their quality color with their tooltip on hover, quests in their difficulty color with a tooltip of level, objective, quest giver and your progress (`ChatFrame.quest_tooltip`). A line's links travel as its `links` ({ name: "item:id" or "quest:id" }); bots write items and quests in brackets, so theirs link too. Slash commands: /s /y /p /1-/4 /w /r /e, emotes (/wave...), /who, /invite, /leave, /help; any other slash runs a developer console command (`DevConsole.COMMANDS`) with its result in the chat. Loot, party and system messages go to the chat too. Bots talk through `scenes/bots/bot_chatter.gd` (the "Bot Chat" option). Every line comes from what the bot is doing (`_things_to_say`): its quest and where that takes it, the named place it's in (the zone's `PLACES`, via `BotManager.place_at`) and the creatures there, what last killed it and where (`Bot.last_death`), the quest it just handed in, items its last kill drops; answers to questions about places, creatures, quests and trainers come from the zone's layout (directions from the zone's settlement, `Zone.hub_name` via `BotManager.hub_of`; a quest giver's place; the nearest trainer of the class). Also "ding" on levelling, greetings when joining, answers to the player's say, party chat and whispers, waving back, and "follow"/"stay" orders in party chat.
- Trading: `ui/trade_window.gd`, opened from a bot's menu (Trade), within 11.11 yards. Classic rules: six slots and money a side, both accept, any change takes your acceptance back, soulbound items can't go (except to a party member, unlike classic; quest items never), and it ends if either side is attacked or walks off. Right-click a bag item (or drop the held one on a slot) to offer it, right-click a slot to take it back, type money as "1s 50c". Bots carry a `BotBag` (`scenes/bots/bot_bag.gd`, saved in the roster): tradeable loot and money from their own kills, 12 stacks, trash and then the cheapest vendored when full. Grey (poor quality) items, equipment included, are trash (`BotBag.is_trash`): bots never offer them, pay only the vendor price for them, and sell them when they pass within 15 yards of a vendor. A trade starts with the item last talked about in chat (`BotChatter.trade_topic`: its ad, a quote, an agreed price) already in: on the bot's side, as many as were talked about, when it's selling, and from your bags when it's buying (`TradeWindow._put_in_topic`). A panel beside the window lists what it sells at its own markup (2-4x vendor price), and clicking one puts it in (clicking it in the trade puts it back). It buys yours at its own rate (1.1-1.4x vendor price), puts in the difference when it owes you, and accepts once your money covers what it wants. Its Trade chat sells what's really in its bag. Trade talk (`BotChatter` "Trade talk"): "WTS [Item]" in a channel or out loud brings whispered offers from a couple of bots, "WTB [Item]" a price from a bot that has it; whispering a bot "how much would you pay for [Item]?", "what do you have?" or a price ("3s?") gets a quote, a list, or an accept/counter (it pays up to 1.6x vendor, sells down to 1.5x). Whispering about its ad without a link ("i'll buy it", "how much?", "still have it?") gets its price for that item, and a linked item with "buy", "want" or "sell me" is the player buying (only "pay", "give", "offer", "worth" or "sell you" make it a sale to the bot; `BotChatter._player_buying`/`_player_selling`). A bare price is about the bot's trade topic (`_topic`: the item last linked to it or in its own last ad, kept 5 minutes; "buy"/"sell" in the line say which way), and is for the lot unless it says "each" or only makes sense per item; with no topic the bot asks which item. Quoted and agreed prices are kept in `BotBag.quotes` and used by the trade window, which updates if a trade with that bot is open. A bot further away than trade range asks "want me to come to you?" with its offer (buying or selling); a yes, or agreeing a price, sends it walking over (`Bot.come_to_player`), and on arrival it opens the trade.
- Social window: `ui/social_window.gd` (O, the `social` action). Friends tab (`GameState.friends`, saved per character; name, level, class, zone or Offline; add by name, remove, invite) and Who tab (every playerbot in the player's zone with level, race and class, a search box, add friend, invite).
- World map: `ui/world_map.gd` (M, the `world_map` action; Esc closes it), like classic's: a large framed map over the darkened screen, opening on the player's zone with the player's arrow, party dots, the name of the place under the mouse (the zone's `PLACES`), every quest giver's "!" or "?" and the objectives of quests underway (the minimap's `quest_areas`, places to explore, chests holding what they need), each with a tooltip on hover (the HUD's tooltip, moved over the map; `Minimap.hover_blocked` while it's open); a dropdown picks a zone or the World, where the zones lie as in the world and clicking one opens it, and right-click zooms out. Its zone pictures are the minimap's (`Minimap.map_texture`, rendered on demand, one at a time, `map_ready` when done). Menu buttons: `ui/micro_menu.gd`, right of the action bar (wrapping to a second row above when there's no room), classic's micro menu: Character Info, Spellbook & Abilities, Talents, Quest Log, Socials, Party Bar, World Map and Main Menu, each half an action button wide with a picture drawn in code, pushed in while its window is open, its name and key on hover; a click sends its key binding's action (Main Menu asks the HUD, `main_menu_requested`).
- Minimap: `ui/minimap.gd`, top right, north up. It renders the zone the player is in once from above (units hidden) into a texture cached per zone (1024 pixels per 400 yards, up to 4096), swapping to the next zone's map and name on crossing into it; quest givers' markers and the spawn areas of creatures that active quests still need, in any zone, are drawn over it (turn-ins and areas on the rim when off the map; quests to take only when on it), and creatures made by `spawn` as red dots (bigger for elites and bosses, grey when dead, on the rim when off the map; hover for name and level). Buffs sit to its left and the quest tracker below it (`Minimap.corner`).
- Options: the `Settings` autoload holds the Video, Sound, Interface, Chat (text size, background, outline; `ChatFrame` reads them and the HUD rebuilds on a `chat_` change; its size is dragged) and Playerbots options and the key bindings (`Settings.ACTIONS`, saved in the `[keys]` section, applied to the InputMap; new rebindable actions go in that list; a binding is a physical keycode, or a negated mouse button, or'd with Godot's Shift, Ctrl and Alt modifier masks for combos such as Shift-1, labelled "s1" on the action bar; Shift, Ctrl or Alt alone binds when let go with no other key; as in classic the most specific binding wins, so game code checks bound actions with `Settings.pressed(event, action)`, `Settings.held(action)` and `Settings.axis(...)` rather than Godot's own, which would fire 1 on Shift-1 too) (`options.cfg` in the profile folder, or beside the `--save=` file), applies display, window size, monitor, frame caps (Background Frame Rate when unfocused) and sound itself, and emits `changed(key)` for the rest. The world's video options (`Settings.WORLD_OPTIONS`: shadow quality, view distance with its fog, field of view, Environment Detail, ambient occlusion, bloom, brightness, gamma as a color-correction curve, contrast, saturation) are applied by `main.gd`'s `_apply_setting`; Environment Detail sets how much of the zones' ground clutter (grass and flowers, no collision; `World.set_clutter`) is drawn. The Performance tab trades the game's crowd-saving work against looks (also `WORLD_OPTIONS`, applied by `_apply_setting`, each explained on hovering its row, `OptionsWindow.HINTS`): Render Resolution (the 3D view's least rows, `main.RENDER_ROWS`: 270, 405, 540, 720), Animation Quality (`HumanoidModel.ANIM_QUALITY`, the steps by distance beasts use too), Character Detail Distance (`DETAIL_RANGE`, how far small parts are drawn), Character Shadows (Off, Normal without weapons and hair, Full), Cloak Physics (Off, Player Only, Everyone; a still cloak just hangs), Spell Effects (`Vfx.quality`: Off, Low with half the cubes within 25 yards, High), Unit Detail Distance (`Bot.FAR`, now a static var: 30, 45, 70 yards) and Distant Unit Updates (`BotManager.FAR_BUDGET`: 1.5, 3, 6 ms); `HumanoidModel.set_graphics` sets the model ones and calls `refresh_graphics` on every model (group `character_models`). With 200 bots on the dev machine: lowest 74 fps, defaults 68, highest 62. Show Frame Rate is a counter in the top right corner of the screen. The Options button (Esc menu, login and character select) opens `ui/options_window.gd`, one window with a tab per category; its title drags it anywhere on screen (kept there, the `options_window_offset` option, saved as soon as it's dropped); changes (key bindings included) wait in the window until Apply (applies and saves) or Okay (the same, and closes), and Cancel or Esc drops them; add an option to `Settings.DEFAULTS` and a row to `OptionsWindow.CATEGORIES`, then read it where it matters.
- Version: the game's version is `config/version` in `project.godot` (`[application]`), as x.y.z, each part any number of digits (0.1.0, 0.12.105). The login screen shows it and Windows exports take it (padded to x.y.z.0; Windows caps each part at 65535), so it is set nowhere else. It changes only when a release is made (a build for players, when the user asks for one), not on every push: the release commit bumps it, x for major updates (a new zone, a big new system), y for moderate ones (a new class, a feature like creature spellcasting), z for small ones (fixes, tweaks, options, dev tools), judged by the largest change since the last release. Before bumping it, propose the new number (and why: the largest change) and wait for the user to confirm it or pick another. Bumping a part resets the parts after it to 0 (0.4.7 to 0.5.0). The release also adds an entry for its new version at the top of `data/patch_notes.json` (`version`, `date` as MM-DD-YYYY, `notes`: short lines for players, in the game's own names) covering everything since the last release (`git log` since the commit that last changed `config/version`); the login screen shows only that newest entry. Ordinary pushes leave both alone.
- Updates: released builds look for a newer release at start and whenever they return to the login or character select from the game (`scripts/update/updater.gd`, `Updater`, run by `FrontEnd`; the Interface option Check for Updates at Start): GitHub's releases for `BountieHuntre/ai-rpg`, tags `v<x.y.z>` (pre-releases count) with an `AI-RPG-v<x.y.z>-windows.zip` asset holding the `AI RPG` folder. ## Conventions
- Typed GDScript, tabs, tunable values as `@export`.
- The 3D game renders in `GameView/GameViewport` at a whole-number pixel scale, at least `min_game_height` rows tall (set on the `Main` node). The look depends on this low resolution.
- The HUD draws at full window resolution, outside the game view. Its layout is in units of 1/270 of the window height, times the UI Scale option (`Settings.hud_scale`).
- Outlines come from `shaders/pixelart_stylizer.gdshader` (MIT, by Leo Peltola) on a plane attached to the game camera. It needs Forward+. Tune them in `shaders/outline_material.tres`.
- Input actions are defined in `project.godot`: `move_*`, `strafe_*`, `target_next`, `ability_1` to `ability_4`, `interact`, `pause`.
- Combat is WoW-style tab-targeting. Clicks (and the hover tooltip) pick what's under the cursor with `Targeting.unit_at`, which passes through corpses already looted to whatever lies under them. The target ring is a decal (`Targeting._ring`, a ring texture made in code) projected down onto whatever is under the target, so it lies on slopes, steps and rocks, sized by the target's `Visual` scale and growing up to three times as big from 8 to 40 yards off (`FAR_START`, `FAR_SPAN`, `FAR_GROWTH`), a thick band with dark edges so it shows on bright ground; units' meshes are on render layer 2 (`Targeting.UNIT_RENDER_LAYER`, set by `Targeting.mark_unit` and the models) and the ring skips them. Physics layers: 1 is world, 2 is units, 3 is corpses.
- Effects: `scripts/effects/vfx.gd` (`Vfx`, static) makes small combat effects of tiny unshaded cubes that fly out and shrink away (opaque, as the outline shader draws over transparent things; `CPUParticles3D`, started only once placed), coloured by spell school (`SCHOOL_COLORS`), and skips anything further than 45 yards from the camera (`Vfx.near`). `Combatant` calls them: `take_damage` sparks for weapons and a school burst for spells (bigger on crits), `heal` green sparkles, `avoid` white sparks on parries and blocks, `add_aura` a ring rising round the feet for buffs (not passives or the other side's debuffs). HumanoidModel's hands glow while casting (`_update_glow`): the hands themselves light up in the spell's school colour, pulsing (the character shader's `hand_glow`, on the hands' colour slot), and cubes drift up from each hand (`Vfx.hand_glow` on BoneAttachment3Ds of the hand bones); creatures' spells come from `CreatureAI.casting`, and bursts on release (`Vfx.release`); creature spells that fly (`Speed`) show a bolt (`Vfx.bolt`, a picture only: CreatureAI's missiles deal the damage). Player spells hit at once, as before, so they have no bolt.
- World sounds (hits, swings, spells) go through `Sfx.play_at(name, position)`: full volume within 4 yards of the player, fading (`Sfx.falloff`) to silence at 40. Interface sounds use `Sfx.play`. The base level is `Sfx.VOLUME_DB`. Floating combat text is only the player's own fight: hits dealt and taken, misses either way, heals on the player, stuns on the player or their target (`CombatHud._involves_player`).
- Physics interpolation is on (`physics/common/physics_interpolation`): units move on the physics step (60 a second) and are drawn smoothly between steps at any frame rate (without it, at 75 frames a second, everything moving stopped every fifth frame, and capes jolted). Move bodies, the camera and anything that follows them in `_physics_process`; things placed every drawn frame (the target ring, effects, bolts) set `physics_interpolation_mode` to off and read where units are drawn with `get_global_transform_interpolated()`, as the name labels and floating combat text do.
- World units are yards. Movement follows classic: run 7, backpedal 4.5, walk 2.5, turn 180°/s, no acceleration or air control. Other keys: M world map, Space jump, Num Lock autorun, Numpad / walk, / opens the chat with a slash typed (reserved, like Esc and `), Enter opens the chat, B bags, C character.
- Items live in `GameState` (equipment, bags, containers); `scripts/items/inventory.gd` holds the classic bag and equip rules. Anything that stacks stacks to 20 (`ItemDB.MAX_STACK`, set when the item data loads; unlike classic's 5 to 200). Ranged weapons fire with Shoot (`SpellDB.SHOOT`, classic's wand Shoot id, known by everyone like Attack and toggled the same way, `SpellCaster.toggle_shoot`; in the spellbook, not put on new characters' bars): `Combatant.start_shooting` repeats shots at the weapon's speed (`ranged_weapon` from `Inventory.gear_totals`/`ranged_weapon`, set by `CharacterStats` with `ranged_attack_power`: level + Agility - 10 for fighters, twice for Rangers, Agility - 10 for the rest, CMaNGOS), the first after half a second, only while standing still, facing and in reach (bows, guns and crossbows 35 yards, thrown weapons and wands 30, all but wands with an 8-yard dead zone, `Inventory.RANGED_REACH`); each shot flies (`Projectile`) and lands as `_land_shot`: physical shots roll miss, block and crit (`CombatRules.roll_ranged`; no dodge or parry) with ranged attack power and armour, wands are spells of their damage school (a resist roll, spell crit). Attack and Shoot exclude each other. Cleric bots holding a wand shoot it from range once their mana is low (`Bot._shoots_wand`). There's no ammo: arrows and shot (item class 6) are left out of items, vendors, loot, quest rewards and kits by `extract.py` (`ITEM_CLASS_PROJECTILE`), thrown weapons are single weapons (sold one at a time, one in kits), and saved stacks of items no longer in the game are dropped on load (`GameState._read_stack`). Stats from gear flow into `CharacterStats` via `GameState.inventory_changed`.
