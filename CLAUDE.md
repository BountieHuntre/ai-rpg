# AI RPG

3D pixel art action RPG built with Godot 4.6 (Forward+, Jolt physics).

## Running
- Editor/game: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64.exe`
- Console build, for logs and scripted checks: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64_console.exe`
- Run the game: `<console exe> --path C:/ai-rpg`
- Re-import after adding files: `<console exe> --headless --path C:/ai-rpg --import`

## Layout
- `scenes/<area>/`: scenes with their scripts beside them (`main`, `player`, `enemies`, `levels`, `camera`)
- `scripts/combat/`: shared combat logic (`Combatant`, `AbilityCaster`, `Targeting`, `Ability`)
- `data/abilities/`: one `Ability` resource per ability
- `scripts/stats/`, `scripts/items/`, `data/classic/`: classic WoW 1.12 stats, combat table, XP, creatures, items and loot, with exact tables and formulas from the CMaNGOS and vmangos server code (see `data/classic/SOURCE.md`). Keep new rules faithful to classic and note the source. `tools/classic_db/extract.py` regenerates the JSON from the CMaNGOS classic database.
- Spells: `SpellDB` reads `data/classic/spells.json`; `SpellCaster` (on the Player) casts with classic checks, GCD, cooldowns and cast times, and holds each spell's effect code following CMaNGOS. Buffs are `Combatant.auras`. `GameState.known_spells` and `action_bar` are saved. UI: `ui/spell_ui.gd` (action bar 1-0,-,=; spellbook P; buff bar). Four extra action bars can be turned on under Interface (`bar_bottom_left`, `bar_bottom_right`, `bar_right`, `bar_right_2`; `GameState.EXTRA_BARS`): the bottom two stack above the XP bar (the stance and cast bars move up), the right two are columns under the minimap (slots shrink to fit, and the quest tracker and bags move left). Their slots follow the main bar's in `GameState.action_bar` (60 in all, older saves padded), and their buttons are key binding actions `bottom_left_1`... (unbound by default, added to the InputMap by `Settings`); `GameState.slot_action`/`shown_slots` map slots to actions and to the bars shown. New spells go in the first empty slot of a shown bar. Spells are learned from an `Npc` with a `trainer` via `ui/trainer_window.gd`: Sir Aldric Fenn trains Templars ("paladin"), Sister Maud Ellery Clerics ("priest"), Sergeant Bram Holloway (by the smithy) Men-at-Arms ("warrior"); a trainer only teaches its own class (`SpellDB.TRAINER_CLASSES`, which also says which classes have spells). The Cleric list merges classic's five racial priest trainers (`CLASS_TRAINERS` in `extract.py`); racial spells carry `races` and `SpellDB.trainer_list(trainer, race)` leaves out other races'. Every Man-at-Arms, Templar and Cleric spell works except those needing another player (`NEEDS_PARTY`) or a missing system (`NOT_YET`: Dominate Will, Borrowed Eyes, Font of Light); first ranks that are talents in classic, racial first ranks and class-quest spells (Bastion and Fury Stance, Goad) are sold free (`TALENT_FIRST_RANKS`, `RACIAL_FIRST_RANKS`, `QUEST_SPELLS` in `extract.py`). The Man-at-Arms (classic Warrior) is in `scripts/spells/warrior_abilities.gd`, a child of the SpellCaster: rage (`Combatant.power_type`; rage spells' costs are tenths; earned from white hits dealt and damage taken by CMaNGOS's RewardRage formula, fading 3 a tick out of combat, never refilled like mana), stances (shapeshift auras on the stance bar, each with its passive via `SPELL_LINKS`; changing stance loses rage; new Men-at-Arms start in Vanguard Stance; abilities need the stances in their `Stances` mask), next-swing attacks (`Combatant.swing_replacer`), Rush and Cut Off, reactive strikes (Riposte Strike after a dodge, Retort after you avoid), shields, area attacks, shouts and taunts (`Enemy.taunt`, `AURA_MOD_TAUNT`). Two-Weapon Fighting lets one-handers go in the off hand, which swings on its own timer for half damage with classic's dual-wield miss. Spells another spell casts that the data doesn't link (Blessed Burst's heal, Withering Touch's and Umbral Guard's strikes) are `SPELL_LINKS` there (`SpellDB.linked_spell`). `SpellCaster` handles effects by kind (heals, damage of any school, buffs, debuffs with a hit roll, channels that moving or another spell ends, absorbs, fear that makes creatures flee, dispels) with the odd ones by name; the matching aura types (stats, dodge, speed, threat, aggro range, damage and healing percentages, heal/leech/trigger ticks) are read in `Combatant`, `CharacterStats` and `Enemy`. Combat-driven effects (Oath procs at classic procs-per-minute, Verdict debuffs that heal or restore, damage shields, hallowed ground, Umbral Guard and Withering Touch striking back, Inner Flame charges, Leeching Embrace) are in `scripts/spells/spell_procs.gd`; periodic aura ticks in `Combatant`. New ranks replace older ranks on the action bar (unlike classic). Templar Presences (classic paladin auras) get their own stance bar above the action bar, filled automatically in the order first learned. Tooltip descriptions for spells (and item Use:/Equip: lines) come from `scripts/spells/spell_text.gd`, written in the game's own words with numbers from the spell data; give a new spell a line there.
- NPCs: `scenes/npcs/npc.tscn` with `trainer` and/or `vendor` (a creature entry in `data/classic/vendors.json`). Vendors buy, sell (right-click bag items) and buy back, following CMaNGOS. Food and drink are item "Use:" spells handled by `SpellCaster.use_item`; X sits.
- Names are the game's own, not classic's: `tools/classic_db/renames.py` maps every classic item, creature, NPC and spell name to an original one when the data is exported (plain words like Wolf and Human stay). Class names are the game's own too (`ClassicData.CLASS_NAMES`: Man-at-Arms, Templar, Ranger, Cutpurse, Cleric, Seer, Magus, Hexer, Wildwarden); code and data keep classic's names for the enum and the "paladin" trainer. Code that spots spell kinds by name uses the new patterns: `Oath of` seals, `Verdict` judgements, `Boon of` blessings, `... Presence` auras.
- World: `scenes/levels/ashford_vale/` is the starting area (levels 1-6), about 400 yards across, built in code from the layout constants in `ashford_vale.gd`: terrain from a height function (`scenes/levels/terrain.gd`), stream, roads, the priory, mine, brigand camp, bridge, seeded trees and rocks (`scenes/levels/props.gd`), and creature spawn groups. NPCs, sky and sun are in its `.tscn`. Saves store the zone; anything else starts at `AshfordVale.SPAWN`. Enemy node names come from the spawn layout, so changing `SPAWNS` orphans saved corpses (harmless).
- Quests: `QuestDB` joins each quest's classic rules (`data/classic/quests.json`, exported from quest_template for the ids in `extract.py`'s `QUESTS`) with the game's own title, text, giver and ender (`data/quests.json`; givers are NPC node names). `QuestLog` has the rules (taking, kill credit, quest-only drops, turning in, classic quest XP); progress is `GameState.quests` and `quests_done`, saved. UI: `ui/quest_window.gd` (talking to an NPC with quests), `ui/quest_log_ui.gd` (L), the tracker and the "!"/"?" markers in `CombatHud`. To add a quest, add its classic entry to `QUESTS`, re-export, and write its text in `data/quests.json`.
- Enemies are set by `creature_entry` (a classic creature_template id); their stats, loot, money, respawn time and wander distance come from the data. Their placeholder looks are in `Enemy.LOOKS`. Whether they attack on sight comes from their classic faction (`data/classic/factions.json`, rules in `ClassicData`): neutral ones and hostile ones flagged no-aggro only fight back, and all passive creatures show yellow. A zone can make creatures passive regardless (`Enemy.passive`; Ashford Vale's `PASSIVE_CREATURES` covers every burrowling).
- Playerbots: computer-run player characters (`scenes/bots/bot.gd`, made by `BotManager` from the Playerbots options). The player and bots are both `PlayerUnit`s, and each has a `CharacterProfile` (name, race, class, level, XP, gear, known spells; `GameState` extends it for the player), which `CharacterStats`, `Progression` and `SpellCaster` read, so bots follow the same rules and cast the same spells (`PlayerUnit.enemy_target()`/`friendly_target()` pick the targets). On their own, bots do quests (the "Random Bots Do Quests" option): `BotQuests` (`scenes/bots/bot_quests.gd`) is a bot's own quest log with the player's classic rules, up to three at a time, saved in its roster entry; bots walk to givers to take and hand in quests (quest XP for their level), hunt the creatures their quests need first, count their own kills, and roll each creature's classic drop chance for quest items (`LootTable.drop_chance`), since they don't loot. Otherwise they hunt creatures near their level, eat and drink when low, and get up 30 s after dying (at the priory when on their own). They go in and out of walled places by the gate: the zone's `ENCLOSURES` and `BotManager.route`. Templar bots heal, keep a Presence, an Oath and Boons up, and use Revival. Man-at-Arms bots take a stance (Bastion with a shield in a party, else Vanguard), Rush in, shout Rallying Cry in fights, and use finishers, reactive strikes, Gash, area attacks when crowded and Mighty Blow with spare rage. Cleric bots heal (a Ward first when someone is in trouble, Communal Mending for three hurt), cure diseases, raise the fallen, keep Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit and their own buffs up, and fight from 25 yards with Gnawing Shadow, Mind Lash and Chastise while they have mana. The "Classes With Spells" bot class option makes only Men-at-Arms, Templars and Clerics. The roster is `playerbots.json` in the profile folder, shared by all characters: name, race, class, level, XP, and each bot's state (`Bot.saved_state()`: zone, position, facing, health, mana, death, a Stay order, its own buffs), saved with the game, so bots carry on where they were. Creatures keep a classic threat table (`Enemy.threat`, heals draw half their amount) and the first player unit to hit one taps it (`tapped_by`); only the player's group gets XP, quest credit and loot from its taps.
- Bot performance: bots more than 45 yards from the player (`Bot.FAR`, not in the party or a trade) are ticked by the `BotManager` in six slices (about 10 times a second each, `Bot.far_tick`) with their nodes' own per-frame work switched off (`Bot.set_far`), and walk the ground's height without collision; they think less often, and re-check quest needs and quest givers every 2 s. Creatures look for players to attack 5 times a second. The Random Bots option goes up to 250: measured on the dev machine, 200 bots hold 65-70 fps, 250 about 55-60, 300 about 40, and from 400 the physics falls behind and the game drops under 10 fps.
- Party: the `Party` autoload holds up to four bots with the player (`GameState.party` saves their names). Clicking a bot in the world only targets it; right-click the target frame (or a party frame, or its name in chat) for its menu: whisper, invite, trade, or tell a member to follow or stay. Bots keep their own level in the party. With "Teleport to Me When Left Behind" on, a following bot teleports beside you when it's over 40 yards away (even mid-fight) or has gained no ground for 4 s while over 12 yards back; bots told to Stay don't. Quest XP the player earns goes to every party bot too, the same amount (`Party.share_quest_xp`, unlike classic). Kill XP is shared by `Group::RewardGroupAtKill` rules (members alive within 74 yards, by level, with the group bonus), and looted money is split. Party frames are under the player frame, with each member's health, mana and XP; members show on the minimap. The target frame shows a mana bar for targets with mana (creatures have their classic mana).
- Chat: the `Chat` autoload holds the log (kept across the reload after death, not saved) and classic's colors and channels (1. General, 2. Trade, 3. LocalDefense, 4. LookingForGroup; General and LocalDefense are per zone, though the tag doesn't name it); `Chat.say` only reaches the player within say (25 yd) or yell (300 yd) range. `ui/chat_frame.gd` is the box in the bottom-left, with tabs: General (every channel, can't be closed) plus any the player adds with "+"; right-click a tab for New Tab, Edit Tab (name and which kinds of line it shows, `ChatFrame.CATEGORIES`), Close Tab and Lock/Unlock Chat; unlocked, drag a tab to move it and the corner grip to resize it. Tabs, place, size and lock are saved as `chatframe_tabs`, `chatframe_rect` and `chatframe_locked` in the options. Enter to type, Esc to cancel, Up/Down for history, sticky channels, "/p " and the like switch the channel as you type, click a name to whisper, right-click it for the bot's menu (as on its frame). Links: shift-click an item in the bags or character window, a quest in the quest log, or a link in the chat to put "[Name]" in the line; any "[Name]" naming a real item or a quest's title is linked (`Chat.link_text`), items in their quality color with their tooltip on hover, quests in their difficulty color with a tooltip of level, objective, quest giver and your progress (`ChatFrame.quest_tooltip`). A line's links travel as its `links` ({ name: "item:id" or "quest:id" }); bots write items and quests in brackets, so theirs link too. Slash commands: /s /y /p /1-/4 /w /r /e, emotes (/wave...), /who, /invite, /leave, /help; any other slash runs a developer console command (`DevConsole.COMMANDS`) with its result in the chat. Loot, party and system messages go to the chat too. Bots talk through `scenes/bots/bot_chatter.gd` (the "Bot Chat" option). Every line comes from what the bot is doing (`_things_to_say`): its quest and where that takes it, the named place it's in (the zone's `PLACES`, via `BotManager.place_at`) and the creatures there, what last killed it and where (`Bot.last_death`), the quest it just handed in, items its last kill drops; answers to questions about places, creatures, quests and trainers come from the zone's layout. Also "ding" on levelling, greetings when joining, answers to the player's say, party chat and whispers, waving back, and "follow"/"stay" orders in party chat.
- Trading: `ui/trade_window.gd`, opened from a bot's menu (Trade), within 11.11 yards. Classic rules: six slots and money a side, both accept, any change takes your acceptance back, soulbound items can't go, and it ends if either side is attacked or walks off. Right-click a bag item (or drop the held one on a slot) to offer it, right-click a slot to take it back, type money as "1s 50c". Bots carry a `BotBag` (`scenes/bots/bot_bag.gd`, saved in the roster): tradeable loot and money from their own kills, 12 stacks, the cheapest vendored when full. In a trade the bot's side starts empty: a panel beside the window lists what it sells at its own markup (2-4x vendor price), and clicking one puts it in (clicking it in the trade puts it back). It buys yours at its own rate (1.1-1.4x vendor price), puts in the difference when it owes you, and accepts once your money covers what it wants. Its Trade chat sells what's really in its bag. Trade talk (`BotChatter` "Trade talk"): "WTS [Item]" in a channel or out loud brings whispered offers from a couple of bots, "WTB [Item]" a price from a bot that has it; whispering a bot "how much would you pay for [Item]?", "what do you have?" or a price ("3s?") gets a quote, a list, or an accept/counter (it pays up to 1.6x vendor, sells down to 1.5x). A bare price is about the bot's trade topic (`_topic`: the item last linked to it or in its own last ad, kept 5 minutes; "buy"/"sell" in the line say which way), and is for the lot unless it says "each" or only makes sense per item; with no topic the bot asks which item. Quoted and agreed prices are kept in `BotBag.quotes` and used by the trade window, which updates if a trade with that bot is open. A bot further away than trade range asks "want me to come to you?" with its offer (buying or selling); a yes, or agreeing a price, sends it walking over (`Bot.come_to_player`), and on arrival it opens the trade.
- Social window: `ui/social_window.gd` (O, the `social` action). Friends tab (`GameState.friends`, saved per character; name, level, class, zone or Offline; add by name, remove, invite) and Who tab (every playerbot in the zone with level, race and class, a search box, add friend, invite).
- Minimap: `ui/minimap.gd`, top right, north up. It renders the zone once from above (units hidden) into a texture cached per zone, reading the zone script's `ZONE_NAME`, `SIZE` and `SPAWNS`; quest givers' markers and the spawn areas of creatures that active quests still need are drawn over it. Buffs sit to its left and the quest tracker below it (`Minimap.corner`).
- Options: the `Settings` autoload holds the Video, Sound, Interface, Chat (text size, background, outline; `ChatFrame` reads them and the HUD rebuilds on a `chat_` change; its size is dragged) and Playerbots options and the key bindings (`Settings.ACTIONS`, saved in the `[keys]` section, applied to the InputMap; new rebindable actions go in that list) (`options.cfg` in the profile folder, or beside the `--save=` file), applies display, window size, monitor, frame caps (Background Frame Rate when unfocused) and sound itself, and emits `changed(key)` for the rest. The world's video options (`Settings.WORLD_OPTIONS`: shadow quality, view distance with its fog, field of view, Environment Detail, ambient occlusion, bloom, brightness, gamma as a color-correction curve, contrast, saturation) are applied by `main.gd`'s `_apply_setting`; Environment Detail sets how much of Ashford Vale's ground clutter (grass and flowers, no collision; `AshfordVale.set_clutter`) is drawn. Show Frame Rate is a counter by the minimap. The Options button (Esc menu, login and character select) opens `ui/options_window.gd`, one window with a tab per category; changes (key bindings included) wait in the window until Apply (applies and saves) or Okay (the same, and closes), and Cancel or Esc drops them; add an option to `Settings.DEFAULTS` and a row to `OptionsWindow.CATEGORIES`, then read it where it matters.
- Release builds: `export_presets.cfg` has a Windows preset (it needs Godot 4.6's export templates installed). It includes `*.json` and `default_options.cfg`, which exports leave out otherwise; the game reads its data from those. `default_options.cfg` (res://) holds the shipped defaults, read over `Settings.DEFAULTS` in every build (players' own `options.cfg` still wins, and Defaults buttons go back to it). `shipdefaults [keys | clear]` in the dev console or chat writes the current options into it (editor runs only; `monitor` and the chat's placement aren't shipped). `Settings.developer_tools()` is false in release exports: no console, Developer options tab, developer chat commands or Shift+F12, and the `--save=`/`--profile=`/`--resume=` arguments are ignored. `-- --pretend-release` tries this from the editor (test arguments still work).
- To do: `TODO.md` at the root lists deferred work.
- `scripts/autoload/`: global singletons. `Sfx.play(name)` plays a sound, with names mapped to files in `sfx.gd`
- `assets/audio/rpg_sfx/`: CC0 sound pack, see its `LICENSE.txt`

## Conventions
- Typed GDScript, tabs, tunable values as `@export`.
- The 3D game renders in `GameView/GameViewport` at a whole-number pixel scale, at least `min_game_height` rows tall (set on the `Main` node). The look depends on this low resolution.
- The HUD draws at full window resolution, outside the game view. Its layout is in units of 1/270 of the window height, times the UI Scale option (`Settings.hud_scale`).
- Outlines come from `shaders/pixelart_stylizer.gdshader` (MIT, by Leo Peltola) on a plane attached to the game camera. It needs Forward+. Tune them in `shaders/outline_material.tres`.
- Input actions are defined in `project.godot`: `move_*`, `strafe_*`, `target_next`, `ability_1` to `ability_4`, `interact`, `pause`.
- Combat is WoW-style tab-targeting. Physics layers: 1 is world, 2 is units, 3 is corpses.
- World sounds (hits, swings, spells) go through `Sfx.play_at(name, position)`: full volume within 4 yards of the player, fading (`Sfx.falloff`) to silence at 40. Interface sounds use `Sfx.play`. The base level is `Sfx.VOLUME_DB`. Floating combat text is only the player's own fight: hits dealt and taken, misses either way, heals on the player, stuns on the player or their target (`CombatHud._involves_player`).
- World units are yards. Movement follows classic: run 7, backpedal 4.5, walk 2.5, turn 180°/s, no acceleration or air control. Other keys: Space jump, Num Lock autorun, Numpad / walk, / opens the chat with a slash typed (reserved, like Esc and `), Enter opens the chat, B bags, C character.
- Items live in `GameState` (equipment, bags, containers); `scripts/items/inventory.gd` holds the classic bag and equip rules. Stats from gear flow into `CharacterStats` via `GameState.inventory_changed`.
- Start: `scenes/front_end/front_end.tscn` is the main scene: a pretend login (any account and password), character select and creation (classic race/class pairs, starting gear and proficiencies from `data/classic/characters.json`; only Men-at-Arms, Templars and Clerics have spells). Enter World goes through `ui/loading_screen.gd` (a landscape, a tip and a progress bar; it loads on a thread, then fades out over the new world). Esc in game opens `ui/game_menu.gd` (Log Out returns to character select). Running `main.tscn` directly plays the first character.
- Saving: `GameState` saves each character (name, items, `player_state` with position, facing, health, mana) to `user://characters/<name>.json`, with a format `SAVE_VERSION`; login settings are in `user://settings.cfg`. `main.gd` saves every 30 s, after inventory changes, on level-up, death, logging out and quitting. Shift+F12 starts the current character over at level 1. Tests must pass `-- --save=<scratch file>` (one character, skips the front end) or `-- --profile=<scratch dir>` (front end with its own characters and settings) so they never touch the real saves.
- Scratch test scripts run with `-s` load before autoloads, so they must load classes that touch `GameState` (Inventory, ItemDB, LootTable) with `load()` at runtime rather than by class name.
- Dev console: ` (the key under Esc) opens `ui/dev_console.gd` in game, with commands like `additem`, `money`, `level`, `learn`, `tele`, `god` and `speed` (`help` lists them). Add a command as a `COMMANDS` entry plus a `_cmd_<name>` method. `bots`, `invite`, `uninvite`, `disband` and `summon` handle playerbots. While it is open `Player.typing` stops the movement keys. `reload` saves, restarts the game with the same arguments plus `-- --resume=<character>` (which skips the login and enters the world) and quits, picking up any change on disk. The Options window's Developer tab runs the same commands (`DevConsole.run`) from buttons, and can turn the ` key off.
- `main.tscn` uses the WoW-style `scenes/camera/orbit_camera.tscn`, which gives the player tank controls. The fixed `game_camera.tscn` is kept for comparison and gives camera-relative movement.
