# AI RPG

3D pixel art action RPG built with Godot 4.6 (Forward+, Jolt physics).

## Running
- Editor/game: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64.exe`
- Console build, for logs and scripted checks: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64_console.exe`
- Run the game: `<console exe> --path C:/ai-rpg`
- Re-import after adding files: `<console exe> --headless --path C:/ai-rpg --import`

## Layout
- `scenes/<area>/`: scenes with their scripts beside them (`main`, `player`, `enemies`, `levels`, `camera`)
- `scripts/combat/`: shared combat logic (`Combatant`, `AbilityCaster`, `Targeting`, `Ability`)
- `data/abilities/`: one `Ability` resource per ability
- `scripts/stats/`, `scripts/items/`, `data/classic/`: classic WoW 1.12 stats, combat table, XP, creatures, items and loot, with exact tables and formulas from the CMaNGOS and vmangos server code (see `data/classic/SOURCE.md`). Keep new rules faithful to classic and note the source. `tools/classic_db/extract.py` regenerates the JSON from the CMaNGOS classic database.
- Spells: `SpellDB` reads `data/classic/spells.json`; `SpellCaster` (on the Player) casts with classic checks, GCD, cooldowns and cast times, and holds each spell's effect code following CMaNGOS. Buffs are `Combatant.auras`. `GameState.known_spells` and `action_bar` are saved. UI: `ui/spell_ui.gd` (action bar 1-0,-,=; spellbook P, laid out as classic's: pages of twelve spells in two columns, with tabs down its right edge for General and each talent tree, a spell's tab coming from its skill line (client SkillLineAbility, `skill_lines.json` from `fetch_client_tables.py`, exported as `book_tabs`, `SpellDB.book_tab`), and a "Hide old ranks" box showing only each spell's highest known rank, saved as the `spellbook_hide_old_ranks` option); buff bar). The target frame shows the target's buffs and debuffs underneath (debuffs are what the other side put on it; the player's own framed gold; hover for what they do), with its cast bar below them. Tooltips are as wide as their widest line (long single-column lines wrap at `ItemTooltip.WIDTH`), never catch the mouse, and sit left of the cursor where there's no room to the right, and above it where there's none below. Four extra action bars can be turned on under Interface (`bar_bottom_left`, `bar_bottom_right`, `bar_right`, `bar_right_2`; `GameState.EXTRA_BARS`): the bottom two stack above the XP bar (the stance and cast bars move up), the right two are columns under the minimap (slots shrink to fit, and the quest tracker and bags move left). Their slots follow the main bar's in `GameState.action_bar` (60 in all, older saves padded), and their buttons are key binding actions `bottom_left_1`... (unbound by default, added to the InputMap by `Settings`); `GameState.slot_action`/`shown_slots` map slots to actions and to the bars shown. New spells go in the first empty slot of a shown bar. Spells are learned from an `Npc` with a `trainer` via `ui/trainer_window.gd` (Train, or Train All for everything available while the money lasts): Sir Aldric Fenn trains Templars ("paladin"), Sister Maud Ellery Clerics ("priest"), Sergeant Bram Holloway (by the smithy) Men-at-Arms ("warrior"); a trainer only teaches its own class (`SpellDB.TRAINER_CLASSES`, which also says which classes have spells). Godric Vane (the Weapon Master, by the smithy) teaches every class the weapon skills it may learn, 10 silver each (Polearms 1 gold from level 20): classic's weapon masters' lists joined (the `weapons` trainer, `WEAPON_MASTERS` in `extract.py`; each entry's `classes` mask from SkillRaceClassInfo, `weapon_skills.json`, which `SpellDB.trainer_list` filters by). Learning any proficiency spell grants its item subclasses (`SpellDB.proficiencies_of`), skills you started with show as known, and weapon skills stay out of the spellbook (`SpellDB.is_weapon_skill`). Bots know every weapon skill their class may learn at their level (`Bot.learn_spells`), so a Blademastery build's BiS is a two-hander. The Cleric list merges classic's five racial priest trainers (`CLASS_TRAINERS` in `extract.py`); racial spells carry `races` and `SpellDB.trainer_list(trainer, race)` leaves out other races'. Every Man-at-Arms, Templar and Cleric spell works except those needing another player (`NEEDS_PARTY`) or a missing system (`NOT_YET`: Dominate Will, Borrowed Eyes, Font of Light); racial first ranks and class-quest spells (Bastion and Fury Stance, Goad, Revival's first rank) are sold free (`RACIAL_FIRST_RANKS`, `QUEST_SPELLS` in `extract.py`); abilities that are talents come from the talent (the trainer sells their later ranks). The Man-at-Arms (classic Warrior) is in `scripts/spells/warrior_abilities.gd`, a child of the SpellCaster: rage (`Combatant.power_type`; rage spells' costs are tenths; earned from white hits dealt and damage taken by CMaNGOS's RewardRage formula, fading 3 a tick out of combat, never refilled like mana), stances (shapeshift auras on the stance bar, each with its passive via `SPELL_LINKS`; changing stance loses rage; new Men-at-Arms start in Vanguard Stance; abilities need the stances in their `Stances` mask), next-swing attacks (`Combatant.swing_replacer`), Rush and Cut Off, reactive strikes (Riposte Strike after a dodge, Retort after you avoid), shields, area attacks, shouts and taunts (`Enemy.taunt`, `AURA_MOD_TAUNT`). Two-Weapon Fighting lets one-handers go in the off hand, which swings on its own timer for half damage with classic's dual-wield miss. Spells another spell casts that the data doesn't link (Blessed Burst's heal, Withering Touch's and Umbral Guard's strikes) are `SPELL_LINKS` there (`SpellDB.linked_spell`). `SpellCaster` handles effects by kind (heals, damage of any school, buffs, debuffs with a hit roll, channels that moving or another spell ends, absorbs, fear that makes creatures flee, dispels) with the odd ones by name; the matching aura types (stats, dodge, speed, threat, aggro range, damage and healing percentages, heal/leech/trigger ticks) are read in `Combatant`, `CharacterStats` and `Enemy`. Combat-driven effects (Oath procs at classic procs-per-minute, Verdict debuffs that heal or restore, damage shields, hallowed ground, Umbral Guard and Withering Touch striking back, Inner Flame charges, Leeching Embrace) are in `scripts/spells/spell_procs.gd`; periodic aura ticks in `Combatant`. New ranks replace older ranks on the action bar (unlike classic). Class buffs lasting 5 minutes or more in classic (Boons and Greater Boons, Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit, Inner Flame, Shadow Warding, Zealous Fervor...) last an hour (`SpellDB._lengthen_buffs`, unlike classic; short ones such as Boon of Shelter and Rallying Cry keep theirs). "Boons" and "Greater Boons" are flyouts (`SpellDB.FLYOUTS`, ids no classic spell uses): spellbook entries that lead their tab and go on the action bar like spells, and clicking one (or its key, `Player.flyout_pressed`) opens a row of the boons you know, highest ranks, to cast one (`SpellUI._toggle_flyout`; upward from bottom bars, leftward from right ones, right-click in the spellbook). Templar Presences (classic paladin auras) get their own stance bar above the action bar, filled automatically in the order first learned. Stances, forms and Presences also show in the buff bar like any buff; never hide them there (only passive spells' auras, such as a stance's "Stance Passive" part, are left out). The stance bar frames the active one in gold. Tooltip descriptions for spells (and item Use:/Equip: lines) come from `scripts/spells/spell_text.gd`, written in the game's own words with numbers from the spell data; give a new spell a line there.
- Spellsword: the game's own class (ClassicData.CharClass.SPELLSWORD = 10, no classic class has the number), designed in `docs/class_designs/spellsword.json` (its page in the README there). Its data is generated: `tools/class_designs/build_class_data.py` turns the design (abilities with their ranks and text, talents with their values) and a spec of how each works into `data/classes/spellsword.json`, in spells.json's shape (spell_template rows from id 900000 in its own spell family 12, one SpellFamilyFlags bit per ability so talents work as spell modifiers; chains, the `spellsword` trainer list with Templar costs by level and Wardweave Form free at 10 as a class-quest spell, Mail at 40; start spells; talents["10"]; book tabs; form passives as links), plus its player tables (each race's Man-at-Arms stats plus the Human Templar's difference from them; the Templar's health and mana) and starting kits (a sword, the race's Man-at-Arms clothes). Rows carry `Description` (SpellText uses it) and `Icon` (Icons.spell_key uses it). `ClassicData.CLASS_FILES` lists the game's own class files: `ClassicData` merges their tables and kits, `SpellDB` their spell data, so `extract.py` can regenerate the classic data untouched. Rerun the generator after changing the design. Races: Human, Wood Elf, Undead, Gnome, Troll. Its abilities are `scripts/spells/spellsword_abilities.gd` (`SpellswordAbilities`, a child of the SpellCaster beside WarriorAbilities, asked first in `_find_problem`/`_finish`): forms Bladesong (20: +10% damage) and Wardweave (21: 45% more threat, 10% less damage taken and dealt, stronger shields, and 2 armor a point of Intellect read in CharacterStats) on the stance bar; Edges (Flame, Frost, Storm, Warding), weapon imbues whose school damage and effect ride every melee hit (`Combatant.proc_event`), scaled by Bladecraft (a quarter of attack power); Resonance (`Combatant.resonance`, 0-5, built by Edged hits, faded 10 s after combat, shown as five gems on the player frame) spent by Release as the Edge inscribed last says (Flame Burst, Rime Lance, Thunderclap, Bulwark of Runes); the rest by name (Phase Step, Binding Glyph, Mirror Guard reflecting a creature's spell through `Combatant.damage_filter`, Glyph Storm's channel, Bastion of Glyphs through `Combatant.guardian`...). Talents with rules of their own are dummy auras read by name (`_talent`); two aura types of our own (`SpellDB.AURA_CONTROL_DURATION_PCT`, `AURA_CRIT_DAMAGE_TAKEN_PCT`) are read by Combatant. Trainers: Magister Ilsabet Rhee in the priory, Tamsin Vey in Wexford. Bots (`Bot._use_spellsword_spells`) tank in Wardweave with Warding Edge when they hold a shield in a party or are told (the party bar's Form), else fight in Bladesong with Flame or Storm Edge; the party bar's categories are Edge and Form.
- Talents: classic's trees for the classes with spells, from the client's Talent and TalentTab tables (`fetch_client_tables.py` writes `talents.json`; `extract.py` exports every rank's spell and renames the trees: Man-at-Arms Blademastery, Ferocity, Bulwark; Templar Grace, Aegis, Zeal; Cleric Devotion, Mercy, Umbra). `scripts/spells/talents.gd` has the rules: a point a level from 10, 5 points in a tree per tier, prerequisites; ranks are `CharacterProfile.talents` (saved; `Talents.validate` on load), unlearning at the class trainer costs 1g, 5g, 10g, then 5g more each time to 50g (`talent_reset_cost`, saved), and the `resettalents` command is free. Talent abilities join the known spells; passive talents become permanent auras (`Combatant.add_passive_aura`, `aura.talent`), their highest rank only, while their weapon, shield or stance requirement holds (`Talents.sync_passives`, run by `CharacterStats.refresh`). Talents that change other spells are spell modifiers (`scripts/spells/spell_mods.gd`, CMaNGOS `ApplySpellMod`: `SpellDB.effect_value`/`cooldown`/`duration`/`cast_time`/`spell_range`/`effect_radius`/`mana_cost` take an optional caster; crit, damage, pushback, resist, crit bonus and charges are applied where those happen); a modifier with no mask of its own takes `spell_affect`'s, and later ranks take the first rank's. Combat events are `Combatant.proc_event` (CMaNGOS ProcFlags and procEx, with the spell); `scripts/spells/talent_procs.gd` fires talents' triggered spells from them (the first rank's `spell_proc_event` row covers every rank), with Lingering Wounds, Inner Light, Hallowed Recovery, Tooth for a Tooth and Gloomweave written out, and uses up talent buffs' charges. Stat talents are read in `CharacterStats`, `Combatant` and `CombatRules`. Descriptions are worked out in `scripts/spells/talent_text.gd`. The window is `ui/talent_window.gd` (N, the `talents` action). Bots spend their points down a main tree (`Talents.bot_trees`, `spend_for_bot`), skipping `BOT_SKIPS` (Lingering Spirit and Font of Light aren't in the game yet).
- NPCs: `scenes/npcs/npc.tscn` with `trainer` and/or `vendor` (a creature entry in `data/classic/vendors.json`). Vendors buy, sell (right-click bag items) and buy back, following CMaNGOS. Food and drink are item "Use:" spells handled by `SpellCaster.use_item`; X sits.
- Names are the game's own, not classic's: `tools/classic_db/renames.py` maps every classic item, creature, NPC and spell name to an original one when the data is exported (plain words like Wolf and Human stay). Class names are the game's own too (`ClassicData.CLASS_NAMES`: Man-at-Arms, Templar, Ranger, Cutpurse, Cleric, Seer, Magus, Hexer, Wildwarden, and the game's own Spellsword); code and data keep classic's names for the enum and the "paladin" trainer. Code that spots spell kinds by name uses the new patterns: `Oath of` seals, `Verdict` judgements, `Boon of` blessings, `... Presence` auras.
- World: `scenes/levels/world.tscn` (`World`, with the sky and sun) holds the zones side by side in one world, in world coordinates, so bots walk between them; `World.zone_at(point)` says which zone a point is in (the one whose square holds it), and `height_at`, `places()`, `spawns()` and `enclosures()` answer for all of them. Each zone is a `Zone` (`scenes/levels/zone.gd`: its name, center, size, graveyard, hub, places, spawns and enclosures, plus shared ground, water, edge walls, clutter, torch, plank and creature-spawning helpers), built in code from the layout constants in its script, its NPCs in its `.tscn`. Zones have mountains along their edges from the same noise (`Zone.base_height`, `mountain_height`), so where two meet the ground joins exactly; `Zone.PASSES` are lines cut through the mountains on both sides (`carve_passes`) where the edge walls open. `scenes/levels/ashford_vale/` is the starting area (levels 1-6, centered on the origin): terrain from a height function (`scenes/levels/terrain.gd`, built from arrays with heights and vertex colors worked out a row at a time on worker threads (`Terrain.parallel`; the `AI_RPG_SERIAL` environment variable runs them on one thread, for chasing crashes), so zones' height and color functions must only read; tree and rock bodies are added to the world after their shapes, as adding shapes to a body in the world rebuilds it each time), stream, roads, the priory, mine, brigand camp, bridge, seeded trees and rocks (`scenes/levels/props.gd`), and creature spawn groups. `scenes/levels/harrowfield/` (levels 6-12) is farmland over the pass south of the vale's bridge, much bigger than the starting vale as classic's second zones are: 1000 yards across (x -500 to 500, z 200 to 1200, so its north edge meets the vale's south edge), with mountains as deep as the vale's at any size (`MOUNTAIN_START`), ground every 2.5 yards, and its places laid out round named flats (`FLATS` with index constants) that the builders place props relative to. Its places: the village of Wexford (inn, chapel and churchyard, Marshal Edric Thorne), the Coombe farm with its fields and pumpkin patch, the Wainwright cottage, the Old Delve mine (northwest hills) and Copperhollow (a second burrowling mine, northeast hills), the Mirrow Mere, Thornwick Tower (hedge-mages), the Ashwood lumber camp in the east woods, the Pellow vineyard, a brigand farmstead, the south watch and bridge, the Reedford landing on the stream, the west garrison, the mirefin village on the mere's east shore, and the gnoll hills with Grunmaw's camp. Wexford has a Templar trainer and a Cleric trainer before the chapel and, at the smithy, a Man-at-Arms trainer and Hesketh Steele the weaponsmith (classic Corina Steele's list, from `npc_vendor_template`; vendors with a `VendorTemplateId` export that); Foreman Edda Harlow keeps the lumber camp; its creatures and quests are classic Elwynn Forest's. Crossing into a zone shows its name (`main.gd` `_check_zone`). Saves store the zone; the dead come back at the graveyard of the zone they died in (`Zone.graveyard`: the priory, Wexford's churchyard), and anything else starts at `AshfordVale.SPAWN`. Enemy node names come from each zone's spawn layout (with a prefix per zone), so changing `SPAWNS` orphans saved corpses (harmless). `tele <zone>` goes to a zone's graveyard.
- Quests: `QuestDB` joins each quest's classic rules (`data/classic/quests.json`, exported from quest_template for the ids in `extract.py`'s `QUESTS`) with the game's own title, text, giver and ender (`data/quests.json`; givers are NPC node names). `QuestLog` has the rules (taking, kill credit, quest-only drops, turning in, classic quest XP); progress is `GameState.quests` and `quests_done`, saved. UI: `ui/quest_window.gd` (talking to an NPC with quests), `ui/quest_log_ui.gd` (L), the tracker and the "!"/"?" markers in `CombatHud`. To add a quest, add its classic entry to `QUESTS`, re-export, and write its text in `data/quests.json`. Exploration quests (QuestFlags 4, classic's area triggers) name a place of the zones in their text's `explore` ("the Old Delve", "Copperhollow"): walking into it (`QuestLog.check_exploration`, from `main.gd`) says "... explored.", and bots walk there (`BotQuests.place_to_explore`, `Bot._check_explored`). Items with a classic `startquest` (the Coin Collection Ledger) offer their quest when used from the bags ("This Item Begins a Quest" on their tooltip; `QuestWindow.open_item`), and accepting takes the item unless the quest needs it; bots don't start these. Game objects (`data/classic/gameobjects.json`, from `GAMEOBJECTS` in `extract.py`; zones place them by `quest_objects` and `chests`, `Zone.spawn_objects`): quest givers and enders that are things are `QuestObject`s (`scenes/objects/quest_object.gd`, an `Npc` with a body's look, its quest marker and no nameplate; A half-eaten body, Aldous's corpse), named by node like any NPC; chests are `GameObject`s (`scenes/objects/game_object.gd`, on the corpse layer: right-click opens them like a corpse in the loot window, `LootTable.roll_object`), which sparkle and open only while they hold something a quest underway needs, and are gone until their classic respawn time once emptied; bots fetch from them (`Bot._fetch_from_chest`).
- Enemies are set by `creature_entry` (a classic creature_template id); their stats, loot, money, respawn time (half classic's, `Enemy.RESPAWN_SCALE`) and wander distance come from the data. Their placeholder looks are in `Enemy.LOOKS`. Whether they attack on sight comes from their classic faction (`data/classic/factions.json`, rules in `ClassicData`): neutral ones and hostile ones flagged no-aggro only fight back, and all passive creatures show yellow. A zone can make creatures passive regardless (`Enemy.passive`; Ashford Vale's `PASSIVE_CREATURES` covers every burrowling). Creatures cast their classic spells from their EventAI script (`creature_ai_scripts`, exported as each creature's `Events`; only cast and ranged-mode actions): `scenes/enemies/creature_ai.gd` (`Enemy.ai`, made for creatures with events) runs the timed, aggro, spawn, range, missing-buff and behind-the-target events, casts with cast times, mana (or a refilling 100 energy), cooldowns, pushback and missile travel, scales damage and mana cost to the creature's level (`creature_class_damage.json`, from `creature_template_classlevelstats`), and is interrupted by stuns, fear, silence (`PreventionType`) and interrupt effects (`Combatant.interrupt_cast`, which locks the school; Hilt Strike and Shield Smash call it). Casters in ranged mode stand off and cast their main spell, closing to melee when out of mana, silenced or locked out. The target frame shows the target's cast (`CombatHud.unit_cast`). Ashford Vale's casters: Burrowling Firecaller (Flame Bolt, Rime Shroud), Pickman (Prying Strike), Brigand Footpad (Back Thrust from behind), Ridgetusk Boar (Headlong Charge); Harrowfield adds the Brigand Hedge-Mage (Rime Bolt, Rime Shroud), Brigand Cutthroat (Snap Kick), Mirefin Lurker (Back Thrust), Mirefin Forager (a potion at low health), the spiders' poison, the Ditchjaw Outrider's Thrash and Grunmaw (Prying Strike, Head Butt), and gnolls and Brasstooth flee at low health. Creature auras that fire on their own melee hits (ProcFlags done melee: Poison Proc, Thrash) are `CreatureAI._on_proc_event`; ones that strike back at attackers are `_on_melee_hit_taken`. To add one, put its creature_template id in `extract.py`'s `CREATURES` and name it and its spells in `renames.py`. Pulling a creature calls for help (CMaNGOS `MarkCallAssistanceOnPull`): creatures of its faction within its `CallForHelp` radius (5 yards by default) that see it, aren't fighting and attack on sight join 2 s later, and their pulls can bring more (`Enemy._mark_assistance`); passive and neutral creatures never come. In Ashford Vale only the brigands do. EventAI can also make a creature flee in a panic for 10 s at low health (`Enemy.flee`, "... tries to run away in fear!") or call for help in a radius (`Enemy.call_for_help`); no vale creature has those in the data. Creatures the data has speak (on aggro, mostly) say lines of the game's own (`CreatureAI.SAYINGS`), in the chat as creature speech (`Chat.monster`, the "Creature Speech" filter).
- Playerbots: computer-run player characters (`scenes/bots/bot.gd`, made by `BotManager` from the Playerbots options). The player and bots are both `PlayerUnit`s, and each has a `CharacterProfile` (name, race, class, level, XP, gear, known spells; `GameState` extends it for the player), which `CharacterStats`, `Progression` and `SpellCaster` read, so bots follow the same rules and cast the same spells (`PlayerUnit.enemy_target()`/`friendly_target()` pick the targets). On their own, bots do quests (the "Random Bots Do Quests" option): `BotQuests` (`scenes/bots/bot_quests.gd`) is a bot's own quest log with the player's classic rules, up to three at a time, saved in its roster entry; bots walk to givers to take and hand in quests (quest XP for their level), hunt the creatures their quests need first, count their own kills, and roll each creature's classic drop chance for quest items (`LootTable.drop_chance`), since they don't loot. Random bots (not in the party) leave what the player's quests underway still need to the player: they don't start on creatures to kill or that drop a wanted quest item, and don't open chests holding one (`BotManager.player_needs`, `Bot._can_hunt`, `_nearest_chest`), though they finish a fight already begun and fight back; party bots help with them. Otherwise they hunt creatures near their level, eat and drink when low, and get up 30 s after dying (at their zone's graveyard when on their own). They go in and out of walled places by the gate (the zones' enclosures) and between zones by the pass, joining it where it's nearest (`BotManager.route`, worked out again when a bot is moved off its way, as when raised at a graveyard); a bot that stops getting anywhere takes a detour round whatever is in the way (`scenes/bots/bot_paths.gd`, `BotPaths`, `BotManager.paths`: an AStarGrid2D search over yard cells near it, each found open or blocked once by a physics test of a bot-sized box above the ground and kept, straightened into waypoints; at most two fresh searches a physics frame), hopping only when there's none, and a far bot (which walks through walls) that comes near the player standing in something steps out to the nearest open cell (`BotPaths.free_spot`); quests, quest givers and hunting grounds in either zone draw them across, so bots past the vale's levels head for Harrowfield. Far bots' long steps stop on each waypoint rather than overshooting it. Delivery quests' items (a letter, a pie) count as had once taken. Bots interrupt casting creatures (Hilt Strike, Shield Smash, Hush). Templar bots heal, keep a Presence, an Oath and Boons up, and use Revival. Man-at-Arms bots take a stance (Bastion when tanking, else Vanguard), Rush in, shout Rallying Cry in fights, and use finishers, reactive strikes, Gash, area attacks when crowded and Mighty Blow with spare rage. Cleric bots heal (a Ward first when someone is in trouble, Communal Mending for three hurt), cure diseases, raise the fallen, keep Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit and their own buffs up, and fight from 25 yards with Gnawing Shadow, Mind Lash and Chastise while they have mana. Bots are made only as playable races and classes (`ClassicData.is_playable`); saved bots of others wait in the roster (`BotManager._fits`). The roster is `playerbots.json` in the profile folder, shared by all characters: name, race, class, level, XP, its equipment (put back over its starting gear on spawn, `BotManager._restore_equipment`), and each bot's state (`Bot.saved_state()`: zone, position, facing, health, mana, death, a Stay order, its buffs and item cooldowns, saved as the player's are), saved with the game, so bots carry on where they were. Creatures keep a classic threat table (`Enemy.threat`, heals draw half their amount) and the first player unit to hit one taps it (`tapped_by`); only the player's group gets XP, quest credit and loot from its taps.
- Bot performance: bots and creatures more than 45 yards from the player (`Bot.FAR`; bots not in the party or a trade) have their nodes' own per-frame work switched off (`Bot.set_far`, `Enemy.set_far`) and are ticked by the `BotManager` instead (`far_tick`): in turn, each at most 10 times a second, until 3 ms of the frame are used (`BotManager.far_schedule`, `FAR_BUDGET`), so however many there are they cost about the same frame time, and with more each is ticked less often (a step is capped at half a second, so a crowded world's far side runs slow). Far units walk the ground's height without collision; heights come from the built terrain's own grid (`Terrain.sample` via `Zone.ground_height`, exactly the collision surface). Far bots think every 0.75 s and re-check quest needs and quest givers every 2 s (which creatures drop a quest item is worked out once per item, `BotQuests.droppers`). Creatures look for players to attack 5 times a second, checking only the player units in the 50-yard squares around them (`Enemy._units_near`), and bots look for creatures the same way (`BotManager.creatures_near`), both grids made once a physics frame; creatures standing still skip collision. Physics runs at most 3 steps a frame (`physics/common/max_physics_steps_per_frame`), so an overloaded frame slows the game a little rather than spiralling. Measured on the dev machine with both zones (276 creatures, Harrowfield at its full size): 75 fps (its cap) with none, 200 bots about 72, 250 about 69, 400 about 59, with far units ticked about 8 times a second at 250-400. The Random Bots option goes up to 400.
- Party: the `Party` autoload holds up to four bots with the player (`GameState.party` saves their names). The party bar (`ui/party_bar.gd`, `PartyBar`) is a pet-bar-like bar of icons above the menu buttons (`MicroMenu.top`), shown while you have a party unless hidden with K (the `party_commands` action, its menu button; the `party_bar_shown` option), taking no keys. Behaviour, buff choices and trained level are saved in `Bot.saved_state`. Clicking a bot in the world only targets it; right-click the target frame (or a party frame, or its name in chat) for its menu: whisper, invite, trade, or tell a member to follow or stay. Bots keep their own level in the party. With "Teleport to Me When Left Behind" on, a following bot teleports beside you when it's over 40 yards away (even mid-fight) or has gained no ground for 4 s while over 12 yards back; bots told to Stay don't. Quest XP the player earns goes to every party bot too, the same amount (`Party.share_quest_xp`, unlike classic). Kill XP is shared by `Group::RewardGroupAtKill` rules (members alive within 74 yards, by level, with the group bonus), and looted money is split. Party frames are under the player frame, with each member's health, mana and XP; members show on the minimap. The target frame shows a mana bar for targets with mana (creatures have their classic mana).
- Chat: the `Chat` autoload holds the log (kept across the reload after death, not saved) and classic's colors and channels (1. General, 2. Trade, 3. LocalDefense, 4. A line's links travel as its `links` ({ name: "item:id" or "quest:id" }); bots write items and quests in brackets, so theirs link too. Slash commands: /s /y /p /1-/4 /w /r /e, emotes (/wave...), /who, /invite, /leave, /help; any other slash runs a developer console command (`DevConsole.COMMANDS`) with its result in the chat. Every line comes from what the bot is doing (`_things_to_say`): its quest and where that takes it, the named place it's in (the zone's `PLACES`, via `BotManager.place_at`) and the creatures there, what last killed it and where (`Bot.last_death`), the quest it just handed in, items its last kill drops; answers to questions about places, creatures, quests and trainers come from the zone's layout (directions from the zone's settlement, `Zone.hub_name` via `BotManager.hub_of`; a quest giver's place; the nearest trainer of the class). - Trading: `ui/trade_window.gd`, opened from a bot's menu (Trade), within 11.11 yards. Classic rules: six slots and money a side, both accept, any change takes your acceptance back, soulbound items can't go (except to a party member, unlike classic; quest items never), and it ends if either side is attacked or walks off. Right-click a bag item (or drop the held one on a slot) to offer it, right-click a slot to take it back, type money as "1s 50c". Bots carry a `BotBag` (`scenes/bots/bot_bag.gd`, saved in the roster): tradeable loot and money from their own kills, 12 stacks, trash and then the cheapest vendored when full. Grey (poor quality) items, equipment included, are trash (`BotBag.is_trash`): bots never offer them, pay only the vendor price for them, and sell them when they pass within 15 yards of a vendor. A trade starts with the item last talked about in chat (`BotChatter.trade_topic`: its ad, a quote, an agreed price) already in: on the bot's side, as many as were talked about, when it's selling, and from your bags when it's buying (`TradeWindow._put_in_topic`). A panel beside the window lists what it sells at its own markup (2-4x vendor price), and clicking one puts it in (clicking it in the trade puts it back). It buys yours at its own rate (1.1-1.4x vendor price), puts in the difference when it owes you, and accepts once your money covers what it wants. Its Trade chat sells what's really in its bag. Trade talk (`BotChatter` "Trade talk"): "WTS [Item]" in a channel or out loud brings whispered offers from a couple of bots, "WTB [Item]" a price from a bot that has it; whispering a bot "how much would you pay for [Item]?", "what do you have?" or a price ("3s?") gets a quote, a list, or an accept/counter (it pays up to 1.6x vendor, sells down to 1.5x). Whispering about its ad without a link ("i'll buy it", "how much?", "still have it?") gets its price for that item, and a linked item with "buy", "want" or "sell me" is the player buying (only "pay", "give", "offer", "worth" or "sell you" make it a sale to the bot; `BotChatter._player_buying`/`_player_selling`). A bare price is about the bot's trade topic (`_topic`: the item last linked to it or in its own last ad, kept 5 minutes; "buy"/"sell" in the line say which way), and is for the lot unless it says "each" or only makes sense per item; with no topic the bot asks which item. Quoted and agreed prices are kept in `BotBag.quotes` and used by the trade window, which updates if a trade with that bot is open. A bot further away than trade range asks "want me to come to you?" with its offer (buying or selling); a yes, or agreeing a price, sends it walking over (`Bot.come_to_player`), and on arrival it opens the trade.

It renders the zone the player is in once from above (units hidden) into a texture cached per zone (1024 pixels per 400 yards, up to 4096), swapping to the next zone's map and name on crossing into it; quest givers' markers and the spawn areas of creatures that active quests still need, in any zone, are drawn over it (turn-ins and areas on the rim when off the map; quests to take only when on it), and creatures made by `spawn` as red dots (bigger for elites and bosses, grey when dead, on the rim when off the map; hover for name and level). The world's video options (`Settings.WORLD_OPTIONS`: shadow quality, view distance with its fog, field of view, Environment Detail, ambient occlusion, bloom, brightness, gamma as a color-correction curve, contrast, saturation) are applied by `main.gd`'s `_apply_setting`; Environment Detail sets how much of the zones' ground clutter (grass and flowers, no collision; `World.set_clutter`) is drawn. - Version: the game's version is `config/version` in `project.godot` (`[application]`), as x.y.z, each part any number of digits (0.1.0, 0.12.105). The login screen shows it and Windows exports take it (padded to x.y.z.0; Windows caps each part at 65535), so it is set nowhere else. It changes only when a release is made (a build for players, when the user asks for one), not on every push: the release commit bumps it, x for major updates (a new zone, a big new system), y for moderate ones (a new class, a feature like creature spellcasting), z for small ones (fixes, tweaks, options, dev tools), judged by the largest change since the last release. Before bumping it, propose the new number (and why: the largest change) and wait for the user to confirm it or pick another. Bumping a part resets the parts after it to 0 (0.4.7 to 0.5.0). The release also adds an entry for its new version at the top of `data/patch_notes.json` (`version`, `date` as MM-DD-YYYY, `notes`: short lines for players, in the game's own names) covering everything since the last release (`git log` since the commit that last changed `config/version`); the login screen shows only that newest entry. Ordinary pushes leave both alone.
- Updates: released builds look for a newer release at start and whenever they return to the login or character select from the game (`scripts/update/updater.gd`, `Updater`, run by `FrontEnd`; the Interface option Check for Updates at Start): GitHub's releases for `BountieHuntre/ai-rpg`, tags `v<x.y.z>` (pre-releases count) with an `AI-RPG-v<x.y.z>-windows.zip` asset holding the `AI RPG` folder. The player is asked Update, Later or Skip This Version (saved in `settings.cfg`); Update downloads and unpacks it under `user://update/`, and a PowerShell script waits for the game to quit, copies the files over the game's folder (the exe and pck keep the running exe's name) and starts it again. Where the folder can't be written, or off Windows, it opens the release page instead. So every release must be published that way (export, zip the `AI RPG` folder, `gh release create v<x.y.z> <zip> --prerelease`). Test from the editor with `-- --check-updates --pretend-version=0.5.0 --update-dir=<a copy of an exported game> --auto-update` (release builds ignore these), using the non-console exe, as the console one waits for the relaunched game.

The tree is advanced by hand (`_advance`): every frame within 15 yards of the camera and for the player, then at 30, 15 and 10 steps a second out to 35 yards and beyond (`ANIM_STEPS`), twice a second out of view, and far bots stop animating (`set_animating`); this took 400 bots from about 14 fps to 22-32 in the uncapped benchmark (about 18 with capsules), most of the model's frame cost being the AnimationTrees. ## Conventions
- Typed GDScript, tabs, tunable values as `@export`.
- The 3D game renders in `GameView/GameViewport` at a whole-number pixel scale, at least `min_game_height` rows tall (set on the `Main` node). The look depends on this low resolution.
- The HUD draws at full window resolution, outside the game view. Its layout is in units of 1/270 of the window height, times the UI Scale option (`Settings.hud_scale`).
- Outlines come from `shaders/pixelart_stylizer.gdshader` (MIT, by Leo Peltola) on a plane attached to the game camera. It needs Forward+. Tune them in `shaders/outline_material.tres`.
- Input actions are defined in `project.godot`: `move_*`, `strafe_*`, `target_next`, `ability_1` to `ability_4`, `interact`, `pause`.
- Combat is WoW-style tab-targeting. Physics layers: 1 is world, 2 is units, 3 is corpses.
- World sounds (hits, swings, spells) go through `Sfx.play_at(name, position)`: full volume within 4 yards of the player, fading (`Sfx.falloff`) to silence at 40. Interface sounds use `Sfx.play`. The base level is `Sfx.VOLUME_DB`. Floating combat text is only the player's own fight: hits dealt and taken, misses either way, heals on the player, stuns on the player or their target (`CombatHud._involves_player`).
- World units are yards. Movement follows classic: run 7, backpedal 4.5, walk 2.5, turn 180°/s, no acceleration or air control. Other keys: M world map, Space jump, Num Lock autorun, Numpad / walk, / opens the chat with a slash typed (reserved, like Esc and `), Enter opens the chat, B bags, C character.
- Items live in `GameState` (equipment, bags, containers); `scripts/items/inventory.gd` holds the classic bag and equip rules. Stats from gear flow into `CharacterStats` via `GameState.inventory_changed`.
- Start: `scenes/front_end/front_end.tscn` is the main scene: a pretend login (any account and password), character select and creation (classic race/class pairs and the Spellsword's, starting gear and proficiencies from `data/classic/characters.json` and the class files; only playable ones can be made, `ClassicData.is_playable`: a race in `ClassicData.PLAYABLE_RACES`, Humans until other races have models, and a class with spells, Men-at-Arms, Templars, Clerics and Spellswords; the rest show greyed, "Coming in a later update", and characters made before stay playable). Enter World goes through `ui/loading_screen.gd` (a landscape, a tip and a progress bar; it loads on a thread, then fades out over the new world). Esc in game opens `ui/game_menu.gd` (Log Out returns to character select). Running `main.tscn` directly plays the first character.
- Saving: `GameState` saves each character (name, items, `player_state` with position, facing, health, mana, item cooldowns and buffs: `Combatant.saved_auras` keeps a unit's own buffs and ones the player or a playerbot put on it, by caster name, for the player and bots alike, and `Combatant.relink_auras` gives them their casters back once the bots are in the world; creatures' debuffs and food and drink aren't kept) to `<profile>/characters/<name>.json`, with a format `SAVE_VERSION`; login settings are in `<profile>/settings.cfg`, beside `options.cfg` and `playerbots.json`. The profile (`GameState.profile_dir`) is `user://` in released games and `user://dev/` in editor and debug runs (`GameState.DEV_PROFILE`), so development never touches a player's saves; the first dev run copies the release profile in (`_use_dev_profile`), and after that they're separate. `main.gd` saves every 30 s, after inventory changes, on level-up, death, logging out and quitting. Shift+F12 starts the current character over at level 1. Tests must pass `-- --save=<scratch file>` (one character, skips the front end) or `-- --profile=<scratch dir>` (front end with its own characters and settings) so they never touch the real saves.
- Scratch test scripts run with `-s` load before autoloads, so they must load classes that touch `GameState` (Inventory, ItemDB, LootTable) with `load()` at runtime rather than by class name.
- Dev console: ` (the key under Esc) opens `ui/dev_console.gd` in game, with commands like `additem`, `money`, `level` (the targeted playerbot's when one is targeted, as is the Developer tab's Level Up), `learn`, `trainall` (every spell the class trainer teaches at your level, free, and the same for each party bot at its own level via `Bot.learn_spells`), `reset confirm` (start the character over, as Shift+F12), `bis` (the best gear in the game for your class and level, put on where it's an upgrade, and the same for every bot in your party by its own build, what they take off going in their `BotBag`: `scripts/items/best_gear.gd` scores items by the build's stat weights, armor and weapon damage, the build being the talent tree with the most points (`BestGear.SPECS`), whose weapon style wins when a set of it exists: Blademastery and Zeal two-handers, Ferocity two weapons, Bulwark and Aegis a shield, Grace, Devotion and Mercy an off-hand item or shield; also a "BiS" button on the Options window's Developer tab), and the Developer tab has a "Spawn Enemy" row (level, rank, Spawn) for `spawn`, `tele`, `god`, `speed` and `spawn [level] [normal | elite | boss]` (a random creature of the zone's data straight ahead, 8 yards past its aggro range, standing still: at a set level or rank it takes its class's base stats for the level from `creature_class_stats.json`, CMaNGOS creature_template_classlevelstats with multipliers 1, times `Enemy.RANK_HEALTH`/`RANK_DAMAGE` for elites and bosses, which are bigger; bosses show "??" for their level, elites a "+"; `Enemy.temporary` makes it unsaved and gone for good 5 minutes after dying) (`help` lists them). Add a command as a `COMMANDS` entry plus a `_cmd_<name>` method. `bots`, `invite`, `uninvite`, `disband` and `summon` handle playerbots, and `randomizebots` (also a "Randomize Bots" button on the Developer tab) deletes every bot but the party's and the player's friends, roster entries and all, and makes new random ones for the current options (`BotManager.randomize_bots`). While it is open `Player.typing` stops the movement keys. `reload` saves, restarts the game with the same arguments plus `-- --resume=<character>` (which skips the login and enters the world) and quits, picking up any change on disk. The Options window's Developer tab runs the same commands (`DevConsole.run`) from buttons, and can turn the ` key off.
- `main.tscn` uses the WoW-style `scenes/camera/orbit_camera.tscn`, which gives the player tank controls. The fixed `game_camera.tscn` is kept for comparison and gives camera-relative movement.
