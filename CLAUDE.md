# AI RPG

3D pixel art action RPG built with Godot 4.6 (Forward+, Jolt physics).

## Running
- Editor/game: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64.exe`
- Console build, for logs and scripted checks: `D:\Godot_v4.6\Godot_v4.6-stable_win64_console.exe`
- Run the game: `<console exe> --path C:/ai-rpg`
- Re-import after adding files: `<console exe> --headless --path C:/ai-rpg --import`

## Layout
- `scenes/<area>/`: scenes with their scripts beside them (`main`, `player`, `enemies`, `levels`, `camera`)
- `scripts/combat/`: shared combat logic (`Combatant`, `AbilityCaster`, `Targeting`, `Ability`)
- `data/abilities/`: one `Ability` resource per ability
- `scripts/stats/`, `scripts/items/`, `data/classic/`: classic WoW 1.12 stats, combat table, XP, creatures, items and loot, with exact tables and formulas from the CMaNGOS and vmangos server code (see `data/classic/SOURCE.md`). Keep new rules faithful to classic and note the source. `tools/classic_db/extract.py` regenerates the JSON from the CMaNGOS classic database.
- Spells: `SpellDB` reads `data/classic/spells.json`; `SpellCaster` (on the Player) casts with classic checks, GCD, cooldowns and cast times, and holds each spell's effect code following CMaNGOS. Buffs are `Combatant.auras`. `GameState.known_spells` and `action_bar` are saved. UI: `ui/spell_ui.gd` (action bar 1-0,-,=; spellbook P, laid out as classic's: pages of twelve spells in two columns, with tabs down its right edge for General and each talent tree, a spell's tab coming from its skill line (client SkillLineAbility, `skill_lines.json` from `fetch_client_tables.py`, exported as `book_tabs`, `SpellDB.book_tab`), and a "Hide old ranks" box showing only each spell's highest known rank, saved as the `spellbook_hide_old_ranks` option); buff bar). The target frame shows the target's buffs and debuffs underneath (debuffs are what the other side put on it; the player's own framed gold; hover for what they do), with its cast bar below them. Tooltips are as wide as their widest line (long single-column lines wrap at `ItemTooltip.WIDTH`), never catch the mouse, and sit left of the cursor where there's no room to the right, and above it where there's none below. Four extra action bars can be turned on under Interface (`bar_bottom_left`, `bar_bottom_right`, `bar_right`, `bar_right_2`; `GameState.EXTRA_BARS`): the bottom two stack above the XP bar (the stance and cast bars move up), the right two are columns under the minimap (slots shrink to fit, and the quest tracker and bags move left). Their slots follow the main bar's in `GameState.action_bar` (60 in all, older saves padded), and their buttons are key binding actions `bottom_left_1`... (unbound by default, added to the InputMap by `Settings`); `GameState.slot_action`/`shown_slots` map slots to actions and to the bars shown. New spells go in the first empty slot of a shown bar. Spells are learned from an `Npc` with a `trainer` via `ui/trainer_window.gd` (Train, or Train All for everything available while the money lasts): Sir Aldric Fenn trains Templars ("paladin"), Sister Maud Ellery Clerics ("priest"), Sergeant Bram Holloway (by the smithy) Men-at-Arms ("warrior"); a trainer only teaches its own class (`SpellDB.TRAINER_CLASSES`, which also says which classes have spells). Godric Vane (the Weapon Master, by the smithy) teaches every class the weapon skills it may learn, 10 silver each (Polearms 1 gold from level 20): classic's weapon masters' lists joined (the `weapons` trainer, `WEAPON_MASTERS` in `extract.py`; each entry's `classes` mask from SkillRaceClassInfo, `weapon_skills.json`, which `SpellDB.trainer_list` filters by). Learning any proficiency spell grants its item subclasses (`SpellDB.proficiencies_of`), skills you started with show as known, and weapon skills stay out of the spellbook (`SpellDB.is_weapon_skill`). Bots know every weapon skill their class may learn at their level (`Bot.learn_spells`), so a Blademastery build's BiS is a two-hander. The Cleric list merges classic's five racial priest trainers (`CLASS_TRAINERS` in `extract.py`); racial spells carry `races` and `SpellDB.trainer_list(trainer, race)` leaves out other races'. Every Man-at-Arms, Templar and Cleric spell works except those needing another player (`NEEDS_PARTY`) or a missing system (`NOT_YET`: Dominate Will, Borrowed Eyes, Font of Light); racial first ranks and class-quest spells (Bastion and Fury Stance, Goad, Revival's first rank) are sold free (`RACIAL_FIRST_RANKS`, `QUEST_SPELLS` in `extract.py`); abilities that are talents come from the talent (the trainer sells their later ranks). The Man-at-Arms (classic Warrior) is in `scripts/spells/warrior_abilities.gd`, a child of the SpellCaster: rage (`Combatant.power_type`; rage spells' costs are tenths; earned from white hits dealt and damage taken by CMaNGOS's RewardRage formula, fading 3 a tick out of combat, never refilled like mana), stances (shapeshift auras on the stance bar, each with its passive via `SPELL_LINKS`; changing stance loses rage; new Men-at-Arms start in Vanguard Stance; abilities need the stances in their `Stances` mask), next-swing attacks (`Combatant.swing_replacer`), Rush and Cut Off, reactive strikes (Riposte Strike after a dodge, Retort after you avoid), shields, area attacks, shouts and taunts (`Enemy.taunt`, `AURA_MOD_TAUNT`). Two-Weapon Fighting lets one-handers go in the off hand, which swings on its own timer for half damage with classic's dual-wield miss. Spells another spell casts that the data doesn't link (Blessed Burst's heal, Withering Touch's and Umbral Guard's strikes) are `SPELL_LINKS` there (`SpellDB.linked_spell`). `SpellCaster` handles effects by kind (weapon strikes from weapon damage effects for any class, `SpellDB.weapon_strike_damage`, `_weapon_strike`; heals, damage of any school, buffs, debuffs with a hit roll, channels that moving or another spell ends, absorbs, fear that makes creatures flee, dispels) with the odd ones by name; the matching aura types (stats, dodge, speed, threat, aggro range, damage and healing percentages, heal/leech/trigger ticks) are read in `Combatant`, `CharacterStats` and `Enemy`. Combat-driven effects (Oath procs at a flat chance per hit: no rates per minute, classic's turned into the chance at a 3 s weapon, `PROC_SPEED` in `extract.py`, Verdict debuffs that heal or restore, damage shields, hallowed ground, Umbral Guard and Withering Touch striking back, Inner Flame charges, Leeching Embrace) are in `scripts/spells/spell_procs.gd`; periodic aura ticks in `Combatant`. New ranks replace older ranks on the action bar (unlike classic). Class buffs lasting 5 minutes or more in classic (Boons and Greater Boons, Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit, Inner Flame, Shadow Warding, Zealous Fervor...) last an hour (`SpellDB._lengthen_buffs`, unlike classic; short ones such as Boon of Shelter and Rallying Cry keep theirs). "Boons" and "Greater Boons" are flyouts (`SpellDB.FLYOUTS`, ids no classic spell uses): spellbook entries that lead their tab and go on the action bar like spells, and clicking one (or its key, `Player.flyout_pressed`) opens a row of the boons you know, highest ranks, to cast one (`SpellUI._toggle_flyout`; upward from bottom bars, leftward from right ones, right-click in the spellbook). Templar Presences (classic paladin auras) get their own stance bar above the action bar, filled automatically in the order first learned. Auras that stack (a spell's StackAmount, `stacks` in a design) multiply their amounts by their stacks (`Combatant.add_aura`), show the count in their icon's corner (buff bar and target frame), and their tooltips give the total (`SpellText.describe`'s `stacks`). Stances, forms and Presences also show in the buff bar like any buff; never hide them there (only passive spells' auras, such as a stance's "Stance Passive" part, are left out). The stance bar frames the active one in gold. Tooltip descriptions for spells (and item Use:/Equip: lines) come from `scripts/spells/spell_text.gd`, written in the game's own words with numbers from the spell data; give a new spell a line there. Anything with a chance says it in %: procs use `SpellDB.proc_chance` (what's rolled; `{chance}` in a design's text), Oaths give their proc's chance, how often at most and what their Verdict does (`SpellText._verdict`, also the Verdict debuffs' own tooltips and Verdict's with an Oath up), and enchants their `chance` (`Enchanting.proc_text`). A weapon's own "Chance on hit:" spell (spelltrigger 2) fires on its hand's melee hits like a proc enchant, at `spellchance_1` (exported from classic's spellppmRate as the chance at a 3 s weapon) or else the spell's chance (`ItemDB.on_hit_chance`, `SpellProcs._on_proc_event`); none of the items in the game yet have one. Proc spells that stun stun (`SpellProcs._enchant_effect`).
- Spell designs: every class with spells (Man-at-Arms, Templar, Ranger, Cleric, Spellsword) has a readable design file, `data/designs/<class>.json` (`SpellDesigns.CLASSES`), which the game reads over spells.json and the class files (`SpellDesigns.apply` in `SpellDB._get_data`, before `_lengthen_buffs`), so editing it changes the class. A design holds its spell groups (a name and its ranks, each rank a spell with readable fields: school, cast, duration, range, cost, power, attributes, stances, item_types, proc_on, effects with type, aura, value as [least, most], target, radius..., and its trainer entry, book tab, link, proc rule and coefficients), its talent trees, start spells and the trainer entries for spells it doesn't hold. Names for numbers are in `data/designs/names.json` (written with `docs/spells/NAMES.md` by `tools/spells/make_names.py`); every field with examples, and how to add a spell or a talent, is `docs/spells/FIELDS.md`. Seconds and yards are written straight, and a value no classic index has becomes a direct field (`CastTime`, `Duration`, `Range`, `MinRange`, `EffectRadiusN`), which `SpellDB`'s readers take first. Unknown fields and names are warned about (`SpellDesigns.warnings`). New spells take ids from 950000, new talents from 9500. Tags (a group's or rank's `tags`) get free `family_flags` bits of the class's family on load, and a modifier's `affects` or a proc's `family_mask` may list tags and spell names (`SpellDesigns._resolve_tags`). `tools/spells/export_designs.gd` writes the files from classic's data (`-- --export`, overwriting them) and, without it, checks that the files build exactly what classic's data does, listing every difference. The Developer tab's Edit Spells... (`OptionsWindow._build_spell_editor`) edits a class's design a spell group or talent tree at a time as JSON: Apply Live puts it in `SpellDesigns.edited` and rereads the spell data (`SpellDB.reload_designs`, which also clears the caches worked out from it and gives auras already on units their spell's new data, `Combatant.refresh_aura_spells`), Save writes the file, Revert goes back to it, New Spell adds a fire bolt to fill in, and Fill Text copies each rank's current tooltip into its `text`. A rank's `text` is its tooltip (`Description`), with placeholders such as `{amount1}`, `{duration}` and `{cost}` filled from its numbers at the player's level, counting their talents (`SpellText.fill`, `SpellText.PLACEHOLDERS`; `SpellText.describe`'s caster sets `_who`, whose spell modifiers `SpellDB.effect_span`, `duration`, `cooldown` and the rest apply, so generated descriptions count them too).
- Spellsword: the game's own class (ClassicData.CharClass.SPELLSWORD = 10, no classic class has the number), designed in `docs/class_designs/spellsword.json` (its page in the README there). Races: Human, Wood Elf, Undead, Gnome, Troll. Its abilities are `scripts/spells/spellsword_abilities.gd` (`SpellswordAbilities`, a child of the SpellCaster beside WarriorAbilities, asked first in `_find_problem`/`_finish`): forms Bladesong (20: +10% damage) and Wardweave (21: 45% more threat, 10% less damage taken and dealt, stronger shields, and 2 armor a point of Intellect read in CharacterStats) on the stance bar; Edges (Flame, Frost, Storm, Warding), weapon imbues whose school damage and effect ride every melee hit (`Combatant.proc_event`), scaled by Bladecraft (a quarter of attack power); Resonance (`Combatant.resonance`, 0-5, built by Edged hits, faded 10 s after combat, shown as five gems on the player frame) spent by Release as the Edge inscribed last says (Flame Burst, Rime Lance, Thunderclap, Bulwark of Runes); the rest by name (Phase Step, Binding Glyph, Mirror Guard reflecting a creature's spell through `Combatant.damage_filter`, Glyph Storm's channel, Bastion of Glyphs through `Combatant.guardian`...). Talents with rules of their own are dummy auras read by name (`_talent`); two aura types of our own (`SpellDB.AURA_CONTROL_DURATION_PCT`, `AURA_CRIT_DAMAGE_TAKEN_PCT`) are read by Combatant. - Talents: classic's trees for the classes with spells, from the client's Talent and TalentTab tables (`fetch_client_tables.py` writes `talents.json`; `extract.py` exports every rank's spell and renames the trees: Man-at-Arms Blademastery, Ferocity, Bulwark; Templar Grace, Aegis, Zeal; Ranger Beastcraft, Sharpshooting, Woodcraft; Cleric Devotion, Mercy, Umbra). `scripts/spells/talents.gd` has the rules: a point a level from 10, 5 points in a tree per tier, prerequisites; ranks are `CharacterProfile.talents` (saved; `Talents.validate` on load), unlearning at the class trainer costs 1g, 5g, 10g, then 5g more each time to 50g (`talent_reset_cost`, saved), and the `resettalents` command (the Developer tab's Reset Talents button) is free. Ashford Vale's casters: Burrowling Firecaller (Flame Bolt, Rime Shroud), Pickman (Prying Strike), Brigand Footpad (Back Thrust from behind), Ridgetusk Boar (Headlong Charge); Harrowfield adds the Brigand Hedge-Mage (Rime Bolt, Rime Shroud), Brigand Cutthroat (Snap Kick), Mirefin Lurker (Back Thrust), Mirefin Forager (a potion at low health), the spiders' poison, the Ditchjaw Outrider's Thrash and Grunmaw (Prying Strike, Head Butt), and gnolls and Brasstooth flee at low health. Creature auras that fire on their own melee hits (ProcFlags done melee: Poison Proc, Thrash) are `CreatureAI._on_proc_event`; ones that strike back at attackers are `_on_melee_hit_taken`. To add one, put its creature_template id in `extract.py`'s `CREATURES` and name it and its spells in `renames.py`. Pulling a creature calls for help (CMaNGOS `MarkCallAssistanceOnPull`): creatures of its faction within its `CallForHelp` radius (5 yards by default) that see it, aren't fighting and attack on sight join 2 s later, and their pulls can bring more (`Enemy._mark_assistance`); passive and neutral creatures never come. In Ashford Vale only the brigands do. EventAI can also make a creature flee in a panic for 10 s at low health (`Enemy.flee`, "... tries to run away in fear!") or call for help in a radius (`Enemy.call_for_help`); no vale creature has those in the data. Creatures the data has speak (on aggro, mostly) say lines of the game's own (`CreatureAI.SAYINGS`), in the chat as creature speech (`Chat.monster`, the "Creature Speech" filter).
- Playerbots: computer-run player characters (`scenes/bots/bot.gd`, made by `BotManager` from the Playerbots options). The player and bots are both `PlayerUnit`s, and each has a `CharacterProfile` (name, race, class, level, XP, gear, known spells; `GameState` extends it for the player), which `CharacterStats`, `Progression` and `SpellCaster` read, so bots follow the same rules and cast the same spells (`PlayerUnit.enemy_target()`/`friendly_target()` pick the targets). On their own, bots do quests (the "Random Bots Do Quests" option): `BotQuests` (`scenes/bots/bot_quests.gd`) is a bot's own quest log with the player's classic rules, up to three at a time, saved in its roster entry; bots walk to givers to take and hand in quests (quest XP for their level), hunt the creatures their quests need first, count their own kills, and roll each creature's classic drop chance for quest items (`LootTable.drop_chance`), since they don't loot. Random bots (not in the party) leave what the player's quests underway still need to the player: they don't start on creatures to kill or that drop a wanted quest item, and don't open chests holding one (`BotManager.player_needs`, `Bot._can_hunt`, `_nearest_chest`), though they finish a fight already begun and fight back; party bots help with them. Otherwise they hunt creatures near their level, eat and drink when low, and get up 30 s after dying (at their zone's graveyard when on their own). They go in and out of walled places by the gate (the zones' enclosures) and between zones by the pass, joining it where it's nearest (`BotManager.route`, worked out again when a bot is moved off its way, as when raised at a graveyard); a bot that stops getting anywhere takes a detour round whatever is in the way (`scenes/bots/bot_paths.gd`, `BotPaths`, `BotManager.paths`: an AStarGrid2D search over yard cells near it, each found open or blocked once by a physics test of a bot-sized box above the ground and kept, straightened into waypoints; at most two fresh searches a physics frame), hopping only when there's none, and a far bot (which walks through walls) that comes near the player standing in something steps out to the nearest open cell (`BotPaths.free_spot`); quests, quest givers and hunting grounds in either zone draw them across, so bots past the vale's levels head for Harrowfield. Far bots' long steps stop on each waypoint rather than overshooting it. Delivery quests' items (a letter, a pie) count as had once taken. Bots interrupt casting creatures (Hilt Strike, Shield Smash, Hush). Templar bots heal, keep a Presence, an Oath and Boons up, and use Revival. Man-at-Arms bots take a stance (Bastion when tanking, else Vanguard), Rush in, shout Rallying Cry in fights, and use finishers, reactive strikes, Gash, area attacks when crowded and Mighty Blow with spare rage. Cleric bots heal (a Ward first when someone is in trouble, Communal Mending for three hurt), cure diseases, raise the fallen, keep Litany of Fortitude, Uplifted Spirit and their own buffs up, and fight from 25 yards with Gnawing Shadow, Mind Lash and Chastise while they have mana. Bots are made only as playable races and classes (`ClassicData.is_playable`); saved bots of others wait in the roster (`BotManager._fits`). The roster is `playerbots.json` in the profile folder, shared by all characters: name, race, class, level, XP, its equipment (put back over its starting gear on spawn, `BotManager._restore_equipment`), and each bot's state (`Bot.saved_state()`: zone, position, facing, health, mana, death, a Stay order, its buffs and item cooldowns, saved as the player's are), saved with the game, so bots carry on where they were. Creatures keep a classic threat table (`Enemy.threat`, heals draw half their amount) and the first player unit to hit one taps it (`tapped_by`); only the player's group gets XP, quest credit and loot from its taps.
- Bot performance: bots and creatures more than 45 yards from the player (`Bot.FAR`; bots not in the party or a trade) have their nodes' own per-frame work switched off (`Bot.set_far`, `Enemy.set_far`) and are ticked by the `BotManager` instead (`far_tick`): in turn, each at most 10 times a second, until 3 ms of the frame are used (`BotManager.far_schedule`, `FAR_BUDGET`), so however many there are they cost about the same frame time, and with more each is ticked less often (a step is capped at half a second, so a crowded world's far side runs slow). Far units walk the ground's height without collision; heights come from the built terrain's own grid (`Terrain.sample` via `Zone.ground_height`, exactly the collision surface). Far bots think every 0.75 s and re-check quest needs and quest givers every 2 s (which creatures drop a quest item is worked out once per item, `BotQuests.droppers`). Creatures look for players to attack 5 times a second, checking only the player units in the 50-yard squares around them (`Enemy._units_near`), and bots look for creatures the same way (`BotManager.creatures_near`), both grids made once a physics frame; creatures standing still skip collision. Physics runs at most 3 steps a frame (`physics/common/max_physics_steps_per_frame`), so an overloaded frame slows the game a little rather than spiralling. Measured on the dev machine with both zones (276 creatures, Harrowfield at its full size): 75 fps (its cap) with none, 200 bots about 72, 250 about 69, 400 about 59, with far units ticked about 8 times a second at 250-400. The Random Bots option goes up to 400.
- Party: the `Party` autoload holds up to four bots with the player (`GameState.party` saves their names). The party bar (`ui/party_bar.gd`, `PartyBar`) is a pet-bar-like bar of icons above the menu buttons (`MicroMenu.top`), shown while you have a party unless hidden with K (the `party_commands` action, its menu button; the `party_bar_shown` option), taking no keys. From the bottom: orders for the member picked or everyone, Follow, Stay, Attack (your target, `Bot.order_attack`), how it fights (click to go through Assist, Defend, Passive: `Bot.behavior`, else the Party Bots Attack option, `Bot.fight_mode`), its role for a member whose class has more than one (Tank, Healer, Damage by `Bot.CLASS_ROLES`; click for the next, right-click for Auto, the class's usual one: Men-at-Arms and Spellswords tank with a shield in a party, Templars and Clerics heal; `Bot.role`, `current_role`, `set_role`, saved: its talents are spent again for the role by `Talents.bot_trees(class, role)`, and it fights by it: tanks take Bastion Stance or Wardweave Form and taunt, a tank Templar keeps Zealous Fervor and uses Hallowed Ground, healers heal below the Bots Heal Below option and others only below 35% (`_heal_below`), a damage Cleric casts from range to its last mana), Sell (to a vendor within 30 yards of you, click junk, right-click everything in its bag: it walks over, `Bot.send_to_vendor`), Train (at its class trainer near you, `Bot.send_to_trainer`, with the count to learn), Bag, Rest; a slot per buff category of the picked member's class showing the spell it keeps up (`Bot.BUFF_CATEGORIES`/`CATEGORY_SPELLS`: Templar Presence, Oath, Boon; Cleric Fortitude, Spirit, Warding, Self; Man-at-Arms Stance, Shout; Auto marked A, None as the category's letter, `Bot.buff_choices`), click for its choices in a column above the bar, right-click for Auto; then the members' portraits and Everyone, to pick whom the orders are for. Hover any icon for what it does. Bots on their own learn their spells as they level; party bots learn them only when trained (`Bot.trained_level`; they say so in party chat on levelling), paying their trainer's classic costs from their own money and then yours (`Bot.train`); leaving the party, they catch up. Behaviour, buff choices and trained level are saved in `Bot.saved_state`. Bag opens `ui/bot_bag_window.gd` (`BotBagWindow`) left of your bags: the bot's equipment and BotBag; right-click its items to take them, right-click your bag items (`InventoryUI.giving`, `give_requested`) or drop the held one there to give them (nothing is bought or sold; soulbound items pass between party members too and stay soulbound in its bag, marked `bound`, which it never sells, trades or offers, `BotBag.is_bound`), click its items to have it put them on, drop your gear on its slots to put it on there, right-click what it wears to take it off; `scripts/items/bot_gear.gd` (`BotGear`) has the equip rules for any profile. Enchants go with the gear every way it moves (giving, taking, equipping, taking off, trades, `bis`): `BotBag.add_stack`, `take`, `take_at` and `Inventory.add_stack` keep a stack whole, an enchanted one in a slot of its own, and the roster saves them; trade tooltips show them (`TradeWindow._with_enchant`), and picking an enchanted copy from a bot's goods trades that copy (`_picked_enchants`, `BotBag.take_enchanted`). Clicking a bot in the world only targets it; right-click the target frame (or a party frame, or its name in chat) for its menu: whisper, invite, trade, or tell a member to follow or stay. Bots keep their own level in the party. Quest XP the player earns goes to every party bot too, the same amount (`Party.share_quest_xp`, unlike classic). Party frames are under the player frame, with each member's health, mana and XP; members show on the minimap. The target frame shows a mana bar for targets with mana (creatures have their classic mana).
- Chat: the `Chat` autoload holds the log (kept across the reload after death, not saved) and classic's colors and channels (1. General, 2. Trade, 3. LocalDefense, 4. - Trading: `ui/trade_window.gd`, opened from a bot's menu (Trade), within 11.11 yards. Classic rules: six slots and money a side, both accept, any change takes your acceptance back, soulbound items can't go (except to a party member, unlike classic; quest items never), and it ends if either side is attacked or walks off. Right-click a bag item (or drop the held one on a slot) to offer it, right-click a slot to take it back, type money as "1s 50c". Bots carry a `BotBag` (`scenes/bots/bot_bag.gd`, saved in the roster): tradeable loot and money from their own kills, 12 stacks, trash and then the cheapest vendored when full. Grey (poor quality) items, equipment included, are trash (`BotBag.is_trash`): bots never offer them, pay only the vendor price for them, and sell them when they pass within 15 yards of a vendor. A trade starts with the item last talked about in chat (`BotChatter.trade_topic`: its ad, a quote, an agreed price) already in: on the bot's side, as many as were talked about, when it's selling, and from your bags when it's buying (`TradeWindow._put_in_topic`). A panel beside the window lists what it sells at its own markup (2-4x vendor price), and clicking one puts it in (clicking it in the trade puts it back). It buys yours at its own rate (1.1-1.4x vendor price), puts in the difference when it owes you, and accepts once your money covers what it wants. Its Trade chat sells what's really in its bag. Trade talk (`BotChatter` "Trade talk"): "WTS [Item]" in a channel or out loud brings whispered offers from a couple of bots, "WTB [Item]" a price from a bot that has it; whispering a bot "how much would you pay for [Item]?", "what do you have?" or a price ("3s?") gets a quote, a list, or an accept/counter (it pays up to 1.6x vendor, sells down to 1.5x). Whispering about its ad without a link ("i'll buy it", "how much?", "still have it?") gets its price for that item, and a linked item with "buy", "want" or "sell me" is the player buying (only "pay", "give", "offer", "worth" or "sell you" make it a sale to the bot; `BotChatter._player_buying`/`_player_selling`). A bare price is about the bot's trade topic (`_topic`: the item last linked to it or in its own last ad, kept 5 minutes; "buy"/"sell" in the line say which way), and is for the lot unless it says "each" or only makes sense per item; with no topic the bot asks which item. Quoted and agreed prices are kept in `BotBag.quotes` and used by the trade window, which updates if a trade with that bot is open. A bot further away than trade range asks "want me to come to you?" with its offer (buying or selling); a yes, or agreeing a price, sends it walking over (`Bot.come_to_player`), and on arrival it opens the trade.

- Version: the game's version is `config/version` in `project.godot` (`[application]`), as x.y.z, each part any number of digits (0.1.0, 0.12.105). The login screen shows it and Windows exports take it (padded to x.y.z.0; Windows caps each part at 65535), so it is set nowhere else. It changes only when a release is made (a build for players, when the user asks for one), not on every push: the release commit bumps it, x for major updates (a new zone, a big new system), y for moderate ones (a new class, a feature like creature spellcasting), z for small ones (fixes, tweaks, options, dev tools), judged by the largest change since the last release. Before bumping it, propose the new number (and why: the largest change) and wait for the user to confirm it or pick another. Bumping a part resets the parts after it to 0 (0.4.7 to 0.5.0). The release also adds an entry for its new version at the top of `data/patch_notes.json` (`version`, `date` as MM-DD-YYYY, `notes`: short lines for players, in the game's own names) covering everything since the last release (`git log` since the commit that last changed `config/version`); the login screen shows only that newest entry. Ordinary pushes leave both alone.
- Updates: released builds look for a newer release at start and whenever they return to the login or character select from the game (`scripts/update/updater.gd`, `Updater`, run by `FrontEnd`; the Interface option Check for Updates at Start): GitHub's releases for `BountieHuntre/ai-rpg`, tags `v<x.y.z>` (pre-releases count) with an `AI-RPG-v<x.y.z>-windows.zip` asset holding the `AI RPG` folder. The player is asked Update, Later or Skip This Version (saved in `settings.cfg`); Update downloads and unpacks it under `user://update/`, and a PowerShell script waits for the game to quit, copies the files over the game's folder (the exe and pck keep the running exe's name) and starts it again. Where the folder can't be written, or off Windows, it opens the release page instead. So every release must be published that way (export, zip the `AI RPG` folder, `gh release create v<x.y.z> <zip> --prerelease`). Test from the editor with `-- --check-updates --pretend-version=0.5.0 --update-dir=<a copy of an exported game> --auto-update` (release builds ignore these), using the non-console exe, as the console one waits for the relaunched game.

They're painted in code by `tools/icons/icon_painter.gd` (`IconPainter`: a design per key's first word, colors by its variant, on a shaded background with a bevel and vignette) and baked by `tools/icons/bake_icons.gd`: after adding items, spells or designs run `<console exe> --path C:/ai-rpg -s res://tools/icons/bake_icons.gd` (with a window; `-- --sheet=<png>` writes a contact sheet, `-- --only=key,key` bakes a few), then re-import. A key with no PNG shows `unknown`. Unit frames (player, target, party) carry portraits from the same icons (`Icons.portrait_key`: a class emblem for the player and playerbots, a creature's kind by name or type, a token for NPCs), the target's on the right as in classic, and their bars sit in gold-edged troughs; the target frame keeps its height, its health bar taking the mana bar's room for targets without mana.

## Conventions
- Typed GDScript, tabs, tunable values as `@export`.
- Everything made (models, props, trees, plants) is one whole solid mesh unless said otherwise: parts joined with no seams or faces inside (Blender models by the build script's boolean union, `_join_solid`; things built in code by a CSG union baked to a `.res`, as `tools/models/nature/bake_trees.gd` does, thin sheets such as snow or fronds kept as overlay surfaces).
- The 3D game renders in `GameView/GameViewport` at a whole-number pixel scale, at least `min_game_height` rows tall (set on the `Main` node). The look depends on this low resolution.
- The HUD draws at full window resolution, outside the game view. Its layout is in units of 1/270 of the window height, times the UI Scale option (`Settings.hud_scale`).
- Outlines come from `shaders/pixelart_stylizer.gdshader` (MIT, by Leo Peltola) on a plane attached to the game camera. It needs Forward+. Tune them in `shaders/outline_material.tres`.
- Input actions are defined in `project.godot`: `move_*`, `strafe_*`, `target_next`, `ability_1` to `ability_4`, `interact`, `pause`.
- Combat is WoW-style tab-targeting. Clicks (and the hover tooltip) pick what's under the cursor with `Targeting.unit_at`; every corpse catches them, looted or not, so each has its tooltip (Corpse, Skinnable) and a click targets it (`Targeting._picked_corpse`, kept until it's gone; a creature dying while targeted still drops the target, and auto-attack never follows a dead target). The target ring is a decal (`Targeting._ring`, a ring texture made in code) projected onto the ground under the target only: `_place_ring` finds that ground straight down from its feet (physics layer 1) and keeps the decal's box a thin `RING_GROUND_SLACK` about it, deeper only as far as the ground's slope rises across the ring, with steep faces faded (`normal_fade`), so it lies on slopes but never on a counter, crate or roof over the ground, sized by the target's `Visual` scale and growing up to three times as big from 8 to 40 yards off (`FAR_START`, `FAR_SPAN`, `FAR_GROWTH`), a thick band with dark edges so it shows on bright ground; units' meshes are on render layer 2 (`Targeting.UNIT_RENDER_LAYER`, set by `Targeting.mark_unit` and the models) and the ring skips them. Physics layers: 1 is world, 2 is units, 3 is corpses.
- Effects: `scripts/effects/vfx.gd` (`Vfx`, static) makes combat effects in the pixel look: small sprites painted in code (`_sprite`/`_shade`: spark, star, mote, flash, ring, plus, flame, shard, leaf, ray, wisp; 16 pixels, nearest filtering) on camera-facing `CPUParticles3D` quads, cut out with alpha scissor rather than see-through (the outline shader draws over transparent things; particles started only once placed), plus solid unshaded 3D shapes (`_solid_node`: a faceted orb, a gem, an ice crystal, a thin ring) and brief `OmniLight3D` flashes (`light`, High only, at most `MAX_LIGHTS` at once). Each spell school moves its own way (`SCHOOL_STYLES`: sparks falling, holy rays rising, flames rising, leaves spinning, frost shards falling, shadow wisps swelling, arcane stars twinkling; `school_burst`), coloured by `SCHOOL_COLORS`, and anything further than 45 yards from the camera is skipped (`Vfx.near`). `Combatant` calls them: `take_damage` sparks for weapons (a ring on crits) and for spells an `impact` (a 3D `shockwave` ring facing the camera, a bright core, the school's burst and a light), fire hits then `burn` on the unit a moment and frost hits `freeze` it (ice crystals that shatter); `heal` green crosses and motes spiralling up with a soft light; `avoid` white sparks and a star on parries and blocks; `add_aura` a 3D ring rising from the feet to the head for buffs (not passives or the other side's debuffs). Effects about a unit are put on it (`_on`, `_place`: hits, heals, buffs such as Oaths, parries, releases), so they move with it while the sprites they throw out drift free in the world; effects about a place (arrow rain, traps, a bolt landing) stay where they happen. HumanoidModel's hands glow while casting (`_update_glow`): the hands themselves light up in the spell's school colour, pulsing (the character shader's `hand_glow`, on the hands' colour slot), and motes drift up from each hand (`Vfx.hand_glow` on BoneAttachment3Ds of the hand bones); creatures' spells come from `CreatureAI.casting`, and a small orb flares on release with the school's burst (`Vfx.release`). Spells that fly (`Speed`) show a bolt (`Vfx.bolt`: the school's solid body, `BOLT_BODIES` (a fireball, an ice crystal, a shadow orb, a spinning arcane gem...), spinning, its sprites streaming round it and trailing behind, with its own light; a picture only). Creature spells' damage comes from CreatureAI's missiles; the player's and bots' travelling spells (Gavel of Reckoning; the Ranger's shots fly by their own `Projectile`) land on arrival, `SpellCaster._land` after the flight, as CMaNGOS has it.
- World sounds (hits, swings, spells) go through `Sfx.play_at(name, position)`: full volume within 4 yards of the player, fading (`Sfx.falloff`) to silence at 40. Interface sounds use `Sfx.play`. The base level is `Sfx.VOLUME_DB`. Floating combat text is only the player's own fight (on every unit, those entering the world later too: `CombatHud._watch` from the tree's `node_added`): hits dealt (weapon white, spell damage of any magic school yellow, `MAGIC_HIT_COLOR`, by `Combatant.last_damage_school`) and taken, misses either way, heals on the player, stuns on the player or their target (`CombatHud._involves_player`), and mana or rage the player's talents give (`Combatant.energized`, from `TalentProcs`: a blue "+12 Mana", red for rage, and "You gain 12 Mana from ..." in the chat).
- Physics interpolation is on (`physics/common/physics_interpolation`): units move on the physics step (60 a second) and are drawn smoothly between steps at any frame rate (without it, at 75 frames a second, everything moving stopped every fifth frame, and capes jolted). Move bodies, the camera and anything that follows them in `_physics_process`; things placed every drawn frame (the target ring, effects, bolts) set `physics_interpolation_mode` to off and read where units are drawn with `get_global_transform_interpolated()`, as the name labels and floating combat text do.
- World units are yards. Movement follows classic: run 7, backpedal 4.5, walk 2.5, turn 180°/s, no acceleration or air control. Other keys: M world map, Space jump, Num Lock autorun, Numpad / walk, / opens the chat with a slash typed (reserved, like Esc and `), Enter opens the chat, B bags, C character.
- Items live in `GameState` (equipment, bags, containers); `scripts/items/inventory.gd` holds the classic bag and equip rules. Anything that stacks stacks to 20 (`ItemDB.MAX_STACK`, set when the item data loads; unlike classic's 5 to 200). Ranged weapons fire with Shoot (`SpellDB.SHOOT`, classic's wand Shoot id, known by everyone like Attack and toggled the same way, `SpellCaster.toggle_shoot`; in the spellbook, not put on new characters' bars): `Combatant.start_shooting` repeats shots at the weapon's speed (`ranged_weapon` from `Inventory.gear_totals`/`ranged_weapon`, set by `CharacterStats` with `ranged_attack_power`: level + Agility - 10 for fighters, twice for Rangers, Agility - 10 for the rest, CMaNGOS), the first after half a second, only while standing still, facing and in reach (bows, guns and crossbows 35 yards, thrown weapons and wands 30, all but wands with an 8-yard dead zone, `Inventory.RANGED_REACH`); each shot flies (`Projectile`) and lands as `_land_shot`: physical shots roll miss, block and crit (`CombatRules.roll_ranged`; no dodge or parry) with ranged attack power and armour, wands are spells of their damage school (a resist roll, spell crit). Attack and Shoot exclude each other. Cleric bots holding a wand shoot it from range once their mana is low (`Bot._shoots_wand`). There's no ammo: arrows and shot (item class 6) are left out of items, vendors, loot, quest rewards and kits by `extract.py` (`ITEM_CLASS_PROJECTILE`), thrown weapons are single weapons (sold one at a time, one in kits), and saved stacks of items no longer in the game are dropped on load (`GameState._read_stack`). Stats from gear flow into `CharacterStats` via `GameState.inventory_changed`.
- Start: `scenes/front_end/front_end.tscn` is the main scene: a pretend login (any account and password), character select and creation (races listed under Alliance and Horde, `ClassicData.ALLIANCE`/`HORDE`; classic race/class pairs and the Spellsword's, starting gear and proficiencies from `data/classic/characters.json` and the class files; only playable ones can be made, `ClassicData.is_playable`: a race in `ClassicData.PLAYABLE_RACES`, those with a model (all eight now), and a class with spells, Men-at-Arms, Templars, Rangers, Clerics and Spellswords; the rest show greyed, "Coming in a later update", and characters made before stay playable). Enter World goes through `ui/loading_screen.gd` (a landscape, a tip and a progress bar; it loads on a thread, then fades out over the new world). Esc in game opens `ui/game_menu.gd` (Log Out returns to character select). Running `main.tscn` directly plays the first character.
- Saving: `GameState` saves each character (name, items, `player_state` with position, facing, health, mana, item cooldowns and buffs: `Combatant.saved_auras` keeps a unit's own buffs and ones the player or a playerbot put on it, by caster name, for the player and bots alike, and `Combatant.relink_auras` gives them their casters back once the bots are in the world; creatures' debuffs, food and drink, and passive talents (put back by `Talents.sync_passives`; restored as plain buffs they never proc, so older saves' are skipped too) aren't kept) to `<profile>/characters/<name>.json`, with a format `SAVE_VERSION`; login settings are in `<profile>/settings.cfg`, beside `options.cfg` and `playerbots.json`. The profile (`GameState.profile_dir`) is `user://` in released games and `user://dev/` in editor and debug runs (`GameState.DEV_PROFILE`), so development never touches a player's saves; the first dev run copies the release profile in (`_use_dev_profile`), and after that they're separate. `main.gd` saves every 30 s, after inventory changes, on level-up, death, logging out and quitting. Shift+F12 starts the current character over at level 1. Tests must pass `-- --save=<scratch file>` (one character, skips the front end) or `-- --profile=<scratch dir>` (front end with its own characters and settings) so they never touch the real saves.
- Scratch test scripts run with `-s` load before autoloads, so they must load classes that touch `GameState` (Inventory, ItemDB, LootTable) with `load()` at runtime rather than by class name.
- Dev console: ` (the key under Esc) opens `ui/dev_console.gd` in game, with commands like `additem`, `money`, `level` (the targeted playerbot's when one is targeted, as is the Developer tab's Level Up), `learn`, `trainall` (every spell the class trainer teaches at your level, free, and the same for each party bot at its own level via `Bot.learn_spells`), `reset confirm` (start the character over, as Shift+F12), `bis` (the best gear in the game for your class and level, put on where it's an upgrade, and the same for every bot in your party by its own build, what they take off going in their `BotBag`: `scripts/items/best_gear.gd` scores items by the build's stat weights, armor and weapon damage, the build being the talent tree with the most points (`BestGear.SPECS`), whose weapon style wins when a set of it exists: Blademastery and Zeal two-handers, Ferocity two weapons, Bulwark and Aegis a shield, Grace, Devotion and Mercy an off-hand item or shield; also a "BiS" button on the Options window's Developer tab), and the Developer tab has a "Spawn Enemy" row (level, rank, Spawn) for `spawn`, `tele`, `god`, `speed` and `spawn [level] [normal | elite | boss]` (a random creature of the zone's data straight ahead, 8 yards past its aggro range, standing still: at a set level or rank it takes its class's base stats for the level from `creature_class_stats.json`, CMaNGOS creature_template_classlevelstats with multipliers 1, times `Enemy.RANK_HEALTH`/`RANK_DAMAGE` for elites and bosses, which are bigger; bosses show "??" for their level, elites a "+"; `Enemy.temporary` makes it unsaved and gone for good 5 minutes after dying) (`help` lists them; and Ctrl+Alt+click anything showing an item's tooltip (bags, vendors, loot, chat links, reagents, quest rewards) puts one in your bags, a full stack (20) with a right click or Shift, enchanted ones with their enchant, each in a slot of its own: `ItemTooltip.hovered_item` and `hovered_enchant`, `CombatHud._input`, developer runs only). Add a command as a `COMMANDS` entry plus a `_cmd_<name>` method. `bots`, `invite`, `uninvite`, `disband` and `summon` handle playerbots, and `randomizebots` (also a "Randomize Bots" button on the Developer tab) deletes every bot but the party's and the player's friends, roster entries and all, and makes new random ones for the current options (`BotManager.randomize_bots`). While it is open `Player.typing` stops the movement keys. `reload` saves, restarts the game with the same arguments plus `-- --resume=<character>` (which skips the login and enters the world) and quits, picking up any change on disk. The Options window's Developer tab runs the same commands (`DevConsole.run`) from buttons, and its Add Items list (click an item to add it, Shift for a stack) has a search box and filters: category, then type and slot within weapons and armour (consumables by type), quality, a sort (name, required level, item level, quality, item ID) with its direction, and gear usable by your class; the choices come from the items there are (`OptionsWindow._items_matching`, `ITEM_CATEGORIES`), and can turn the ` key off.
- `main.tscn` uses the WoW-style `scenes/camera/orbit_camera.tscn`, which gives the player tank controls. The fixed `game_camera.tscn` is kept for comparison and gives camera-relative movement.
